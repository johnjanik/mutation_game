## 2026-10-26 RT-FIX-P6-atlas: the fundamental position written e_i, the item heading included (P6-RT-08-04)
Decision: every text an Atlas page writes names the fundamental position of a node by the site's term of SPEC Appendix C, as T12 does since RT-FIX-P5-t12-content ("the fundamental position written e_i"): the minuscule sentence ("every number fired in the game from its fundamental position is 1"), the orbit sentence ("The orbit of the fundamental position e_i of each node i, in the order of the nodes, has (...) positions"), the link of the item minus-w0 ("Play the numbers game on T from the fundamental position of node 1 in the Game Board; played to the end it stops at minus the fundamental position of node k") and the link of the item orbit-sizes ("Open the Orbit Garden on T at the fundamental position of node 1 and grow its orbit"). The term is defined once, in the paragraph that opens the sheet: "the fundamental position e_i has the number 1 on node i and 0 on every other node" (8.4 bullet 1). The item heading, which the finding left out because it follows the list of SPEC 4 T14 ("orbit sizes of the fundamental weights"), is decided by the same rule and reads "Orbits of the fundamental positions", in the h2 and in the list of in-page links; its anchor `#orbit-sizes` and the data-out `fundamental-orbit-sizes` are unchanged. The entry of SPEC 4 T14 lists what a sheet holds in the specification's words, as the entry of T12 writes omega_i, and RT-FIX-P5-t12-content read Appendix C and 8.4 as binding the tool's text all the same (its panel title became "The orbit of e1 in layers"). This replaces "Orbits of the fundamental weights" in decisions/P6-A4.md, "the items as shown"; the rest of that entry stands. The sheet gives no Lie theory name for the term: an Atlas page has no Lie theory paragraph (8.2 gives one to the lessons), the glossary row of the fundamental position names omega_i, and the adopted reproducer forbids omega_i anywhere in the sheet's sentences. The opening paragraph keeps "the numbers of a position are its Dynkin labels", which no finding names. What the specification left open: nothing, by Appendix C and 8.4 (the heading's wording within that rule). Reason: finding P6-RT-08-04 and the coordinator's instruction to decide the heading by the same rule.

## 2026-10-26 RT-FIX-P6-atlas: the bridge sentence on every Atlas page (P6-RT-10-02)
Decision: every sheet carries the sentence of SPEC 2.4(a) once, word for word, as a paragraph of its own (`<p data-bridge>`, the sentence in `<strong>` as M0 and M4 set it) right after the paragraph that opens the sheet, before the items: "The number on node i is the amount by which a mutation would lower city i." The two letters i are inline MathML (`<math><mi>i</mi></math>`, A-12), as T9 writes the sentence, and not KaTeX: KaTeX's output doubles each letter (its MathML and its HTML layer), so the text of the sentence would not be the sentence, and the adopted reproducer's pattern, which allows only markup between "node", "i" and "is", would not match it. The opening paragraph gains the sentence that makes the bridge true where it stands: "The translator sends a population p to the numbers a = Cp, and a mutation at node i replaces p_i by p_i - a_i" (2.4(a), entry by entry), so the bridge needs no provenance line of its own and the provenance lines of the items are unchanged. The place is the opening paragraph because the two views are shown together in more than one item (the highest roots and their numbers, the highest short root with its numbers, the translation a = Cp of det C), and the sentence is printed once per page, as the proposed fix asks. The hook `data-bridge` is listed with the other hooks at the top of `site/tests/e2e/T14.spec.ts`; no data-out name is added (the registry's outs are values with data-exact, and the sentence is not one). The pages still ship no script. What the specification left open: where and how a page that is not a lesson sets the sentence (2.4(a) [D] fixes the wording, not the place or the markup). Reason: finding P6-RT-10-02.
