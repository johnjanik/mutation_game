## 2026-10-25 P6-A5: the dynkin form, the arrows and the refusals
Decision: `exportLaTeX('dynkin', { type, black?, tau? })` writes `\dynkin`, then `[involutions={...}]` only when there is at least one arrow, one space, the family letter of `type` (never the rank), and the marks in braces: one character per label 1..n in Bourbaki order (label k is Bourbaki node k, E-7), `*` for a black node and `o` for a white one. An arrow is a pair {i, tau(i)} with i < tau(i) and both nodes white (2.15, 6.4 "pairs of white nodes"), written as the two labels concatenated, smaller first; pairs are joined by `;` with no space, in increasing order of the smaller label. A tau that moves black nodes adds nothing (the 3 and 5 of e6(-14), the swap of so(1,7), the flip of su(4)), so T13's screen toggle "show tau on black nodes" [D] does not change the export (plan reading). Refusals, each a `RangeError` naming the fault: data that is not an object; a `type` that is not one of A1..A8, B2..B8, C2..C8, D4..D8, E6..E8, F4, G2 (affine, twisted, `pentagon`, `square`, custom boards, other ranks); a label of `black` or `tau` that is not an integer from 1 to n; a label repeated in `black`; a `tau` of the wrong length, not a permutation, or whose square is not the identity; an unknown kind. Whether tau is a symmetry of the board and whether the pair is admissible are left to the caller (T13). A pair with a label above 9 throws rather than writing `{110}`; no type of the list reaches it. The package's named diagrams (`\dynkin E{VI}`), its short forms (`\dynkin E8`), ellipsis marks and the `fold` option are never written. What the specification left open: the punctuation beyond the three forms and the refusals. Reason: SPEC 6.7, 2.15; the instruction (5.1). Checked against all 167 records of the golden key `satake` (marks equal `black_nodes`, option equal `arrows`).

## 2026-10-25 P6-A5: the TikZ forms
Decision: `tikz-graph` (`{ nodes: { x, y, label? }[], edges: [i, j][], directed? }`): a `tikzpicture` holding one `\node[circle,draw,inner sep=1pt] (n<k>) at (x,y) {<label>};` per vertex in the order given, then one `\draw (n<i>) -- (n<j>);` per edge in the order given; a directed graph writes `\draw[->] (n<i>) -- (n<j>);`. `tikz-points` (`{ points: [x, y][], edges?, labels? }`): one `\fill (x,y) circle (1pt);` per point, a labelled point followed by `\node[anchor=south west,inner sep=1pt] at (x,y) {<label>};`, then one `\draw (x1,y1) -- (x2,y2);` per edge. Lines inside the picture are indented by two spaces; no `\usetikzlibrary`, no style. Coordinates are in the caller's units (centimetres), y up (a caller converting from SVG screen coordinates negates y), no scaling inside `exportLaTeX`; a number is written with at most 4 decimals, trailing zeros removed, minus zero as `0`; a coordinate that is not a finite number below 1e15 in size throws `RangeError`, as does an edge that does not join two given vertices or a label list of the wrong length. A label is written in full in math mode (`{$1$}`, as the instruction's example), with the TeX special characters escaped in forms valid in math mode with no package beyond TikZ: `\#`, `\$`, `\%`, `\&`, `\_`, `\{`, `\}`, and `\mbox{\textbackslash}`, `\mbox{\textasciitilde}`, `\mbox{\textasciicircum}`; an empty label gives `{}`. Deviation from the plan's wording: the plan says "`->` instead of `--` when directed"; `\draw (a) -> (b);` is not a TikZ path and would not compile, so the arrow is the option `[->]` on the same `--` path. What the specification left open: everything beyond "plain TikZ coordinates". Reason: the instruction (5.2) and the owner's compile check at G6.
