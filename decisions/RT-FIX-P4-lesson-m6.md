## 2026-10-24 RT-FIX-P4-lesson-m6: the round and move counts are golden values; part of "P4-A5: typed numbers" withdrawn (P4-RT-08-02)
Decision: the part of the entry "P4-A5: typed numbers" (decisions/P4-A5.md, folded in DECISIONS.md) that lists "the round counts 3, 4, 6" among the numbers with no golden value, and the sentence "The counts 'three rounds', 'eight moves', 'twelve moves' are words", are withdrawn. Why: a round of beat 2 is one mutation at each of two cities, the two-colour product of SPEC 2.14 on a rank-2 board, whose order is the Coxeter number; so the round counts 3, 4, 6 are `types.A2.coxeter_number`, `types.B2.coxeter_number` and `types.G2.coxeter_number`, and the move counts 8 and 12 are twice the last two (the oracle's alternating mutations return after 3, 4 and 6 rounds, and the golden values agree). SPEC 7.3 and 8.4 say such numbers are never typed. Now: the round counts are `{{types.<T>.coxeter_number}}` inside `<Tex>` in beat 2 and in the Seen box, and `fmt('types.<T>.coxeter_number')` in the props of b3 and b4 (caption, and the question, options and reveal of the b4 prediction); the move counts are the exports `B2_MOVES` and `G2_MOVES` (`2 * gv('types.<T>.coxeter_number')`), which also set the length of the two scripts (`scriptOf`) and of the play behind the b4 reveal (`alternation`), and the prose prints them as words through the export `inWords` (the way `DIFFERING` prints a count as a word), so the page reads as before ("after eight moves", "twelve moves", as `M6.spec.ts` checks). What the specification left open: how a number derived from a golden value (twice the Coxeter number) is printed in prose; SPEC 7.3 names `<G>` and `{{path}}`, which print the golden value itself. Other counts in the same paragraphs were checked for the same cause and left as they are: the products 1, 2, 3, 4 of `A_ij A_ji` (the hypothesis of the theorem, no golden value; written in `<Tex>`), the road counts "two", "three", "one" (they define the board, as SPEC 2.1 and the beat 1 line of SPEC 3 M6 do; the golden adjacency records the input, not a computed result), the start positions (2, 1) and (1, 2), and `rounds={2}` (two moves to a round, the number of cities).
