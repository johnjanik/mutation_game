## 2026-10-25 P6-A2: the order of checks
Decision: `satakeCatalogue(name)`: `boardFromName(name)` (NotABoard on a name it does not know), then `requireGcm` and `requireFinite` (NeedsTwoWayRoads, NotFiniteType), before any entry is built. `satakeRecord(name, entry)`: `boardFromName(name)` (NotABoard), then the shape of the entry (RangeError: not an object, `tau` not a permutation of `0..n-1`, `black_nodes` with a repeated, fractional or out-of-range position), then the guard, then `satake` (NotAdmissible). `canonicalPair(d)` and `matchCatalogue(d)`: the shape of the diagram (RangeError), then the guard, then (matchCatalogue only) the name of the board (RangeError, below). What the specification left open: the instruction says "Decide through guards.ts as the first statement"; MERGE accepted for P6-A1 the engine-wide order "argument shape, then kind, then class", both before any computation. Default used: that order, so a malformed pair is a RangeError on every kind of board. Reason: one order across the public API (P2-A1 to P6-A1). A one-way board without a name still gives NeedsTwoWayRoads from matchCatalogue, as the guard runs before the name check.

## 2026-10-25 P6-A2: error classes of satakeCatalogue and satakeRecord on names

## 2026-10-25 P6-A2: matchCatalogue on a board without a name, or with a name that is not its own
Decision: `matchCatalogue` throws `RangeError('matchCatalogue needs a board built by boardFromName')` when the board has no `name` (a custom board of finite type, a sub-board, the C2 matrix built by `fromAdjacency`, the sum of two A2), and also (plan addition, defensive) when the board's integer matrix is not the standard matrix of its name (`cartan(name)`), so that a hand-made object carrying another board's name cannot match the wrong catalogue. `canonicalPair` needs no name and works on every finite board with at most `CAPS.relabellingsDefault` (10000) symmetries, in several pieces included; on a board with more (only a custom board of 8 or more nodes, for example 8 isolated nodes) it throws `RangeError` naming the cap (corrected in the repair after review; see the entry 'canonicalPair refuses a board with more symmetries than the cap'). What the specification left open: 5.4 and T13 do not say what a board without a name matches. Default used: the plan's (instruction 5.6); the matrix check is mine. Reason: the catalogue is organised by board name (6.4); T13 offers only named boards. Named in `openQuestions`.

## 2026-10-25 P6-A2: satakeRecord fields and the C2 adjustment
Decision: the record is a plain object built field by field in the oracle's order (`dynkin_type`, `cartan_label`, `real_form`, `black_nodes`, `tau`, `arrows`, `tau_moves_black_nodes`, `restricted_type_display`, then the 19 fields of `satake` copied by name), which is also the key order of the golden records. `dynkin_type` is the name given; `black_nodes` a sorted copy; `tau` a copy; `arrows` the pairs `[min(i, tau[i]), max(i, tau[i])]` for white `i` with `tau[i] != i`, each once, sorted lexicographically; `tau_moves_black_nodes` is true when some black `i` has `tau[i] != i`. The adjustment of 6.4 "Names in rank 2" is keyed on `entry.restricted_type_display === 'C2'`, as the oracle's `satake_record`: it checks `satake`'s `restricted_board` is `B2` (else `EngineError('restricted board of a C2 record is not B2')`, the oracle's assert), sets `restricted_board` to `C2` and `restricted_orbits_in_bourbaki_order` to a reversed copy (inner lists copied too). `restricted_type` stays `B2`, and every other field keeps `satake`'s orbit order. The arrays of `satake`'s result are placed in the record as they are (each call of `satake` builds fresh ones). What the specification left open: nothing beyond the plan's field order. Reason: SPEC 5.2 ("records with the golden field names"), 6.4.

## 2026-10-25 P6-A2: canonicalPair and matchCatalogue
Decision: the class representative is the least `(sorted g(X), g tau g^-1)` over every symmetry `g` of `symmetries(board)` (at most 6 on a named finite board, D4's rotations included; asked with the limit `CAPS.relabellingsDefault + 1` and refused above the cap since the repair after review), with `t[g[i]] = g[tau[i]]`, comparing the black lists first and then tau, lexicographically on integers (Python's tuple order). No admissibility check. `matchCatalogue` computes `symmetries(board)` once per call and passes it to the private helper for the diagram and for every entry, and calls `satakeRecord(name, entry)` only for the matching entries (at most two), in catalogue order; it never throws NotAdmissible, since a pair that is not admissible matches no entry. The examples of the instruction (D4 black 3, 4 or 1, 3 or 1, 4 with the identity giving `{ black: [0, 2], tau: id }`; so(1,7) giving `{ black: [0, 1, 2], tau: [2, 1, 0, 3] }`; su(1,3) its own pair) and six more were checked against the oracle's `canonical_pair` by a script in the scratch directory. What the specification left open: nothing (5.5 verbatim). Reason: E-5.

## 2026-10-25 P6-A2: canonicalPair refuses a board with more symmetries than the cap (repair after review)
Decision: a private helper `allSymmetries(board, fn)` in `satakeCatalogue.ts` asks `symmetries(board, CAPS.relabellingsDefault + 1)`; when more than `CAPS.relabellingsDefault` (10000) come back it throws `RangeError('<fn>: the board has more than CAPS.relabellingsDefault = 10000 symmetries, so the class of a pair is not computed')`. `canonicalPair` calls it after the shape check and the guard (order of checks unchanged: shape, kind, class, then this domain check); `matchCatalogue` calls it too, where it is never reached (a named finite board has at most 6 symmetries, and the name check comes first). Before the repair the minimum was taken over the first 10000 symmetries only, without warning, so two pairs of one class could get different representatives (the review's case: 8 isolated nodes, black `[6, 7]` gave `[0, 2]` and black `[0, 1]` gave `[0, 1]`). The sentence 'canonicalPair ... works on every finite board' of the entry 'matchCatalogue on a board without a name, or with a name that is not its own' and the words 'at most 6 on a finite board' of the entry 'canonicalPair and matchCatalogue' are corrected in place. Test: `p6b-guards.test.ts > a board with more symmetries than the cap (repair after review)`: on 8 isolated nodes `canonicalPair` throws `RangeError` whose message names `CAPS.relabellingsDefault` and 10000, for black `[6, 7]`, `[0, 1]` and a tau exchanging two nodes (red before the fix: 'expected null to be an instance of RangeError'); on 7 isolated nodes (5040 symmetries) it gives the oracle's `canonical_pair` on four pairs. What the specification left open: the domain of canonicalPair (SPEC 5.5 says 'every symmetry'). Default used: MERGE's ruling (1), a refusal rather than a truncated field (E-10 reports truncation of an enumeration, which this is not). Reason: the review's finding 1; CLAUDE.md 'Every enumeration has a cap'.
