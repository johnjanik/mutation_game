## 2026-10-10 P3-X1: what the document tasks are (diagnosis)
Decision: record, from DevTools traces at 360 by 800 with the processor slowed 4 times (Playwright, CDP) and bounding experiments on copies of the built M3 page (not of record; single or triple runs, load 1 to 4):
- With everything after beat 1 removed from the HTML, TBT fell to about 26 ms; with everything after beat 1 kept but `display: none`, to about 460 ms (Style & Layout 339 ms against 1188 ms). The cost is the rendering of the content after the first view, not its parsing.
- The time is not proportional to the number of layout objects. While the parser streams the rest of the page, Chromium paints a frame after each chunk; each frame lays out again, and passes with 6 to 103 dirty objects out of 600 to 1500 took 30 to 220 ms each (4 times slowed). Hiding beat 1's tool, hiding every KaTeX formula, or switching off container queries did not change that much.
- Removing KaTeX's HTML layer (keeping MathML) did not help M3; removing every island after beat 1 halved TBT.
- The b1 hydration task (200 to 330 ms, the Svelte runtime) is by itself above the 200 ms line on this container (a 300 ms task alone contributes 250 ms of TBT). No layout or markup change can remove it; only the tools can (SPEC 7.6 fixes `client:idle` for the first tool).

## 2026-10-10 P3-X1: remedy (a), content-visibility: auto, tried and dropped
Decision: dropped. Tried as the instruction describes, through Lesson.astro only: `content-visibility: auto` with `contain-intrinsic-block-size: auto <typical height>` (beats 2200 px, Seen 1000, Lie theory 550, Sources 1300, challenges' tool frames 1100) on every beat after the first, Seen, LieTheory, Sources and the tool frames placed directly in the article; paint containment would have clipped the figures that break out of their beat, so each skipped beat was widened to the figure column by a negative margin and an equal padding (prose and figures stayed at the same pixels: checked at 360, 500, 800 and 1280 pixels on M0 to M3, no new horizontal scroll), with 8 pixels of room for focus rings elsewhere, and a print rule restoring `visible`.
Measured: alone (with the M2 swap built in, load 3.53 to 1.40) M0 420.1, M1 808.6, M2 933.7, M3 1253.6 ms TBT; together with remedy (c1) (load 0.69 to 1.37) M0 332.8, M1 680.3, M2 685.4, M3 1091.4 ms; LCP 0.44 to 0.62 s, CLS unchanged (at most 0.0017). Traces show why the gain is small: Chromium lays out a newly parsed block once before it decides the block is off screen; `content-visibility: hidden` on the same blocks was no cheaper.
Why dropped: the full browser suite failed 4 tests with it (M1 "no forbidden statement" and "writing rules", M2 "anatomy", M3 "no forbidden statement"): `innerText` of a skipped block is empty, and the lesson tests read the Lie theory paragraph, beat 5 and the whole article with `innerText`. Worse, checks that read `main.innerText` (for example "no em-dash in the text") would pass vacuously on the skipped blocks. Tests may not be loosened, and the lessons' tests are not mine, so the remedy goes. Lesson.astro keeps a comment saying so.

## 2026-10-10 P3-X1: remedy (c1), the render hint for the first view, kept
Decision: kept. Lesson.astro puts `<link rel="expect" href="#first-view-end" blocking="render">` into the head (through a new named slot `head` of Base.astro), and Beat.astro writes `<div id="first-view-end" hidden></div>` at the end of beat 1. A browser that knows the hint (Chromium 124 and later) paints once the end of beat 1 has been parsed instead of painting and laying out the first view again for every chunk of HTML; others ignore it; if the marker is missing the browser paints when the document has been parsed. Nothing changes without JavaScript (it is markup), nothing is per lesson, and M4 and M5 get it by using Beat as the anatomy says.
Measured: alone, without (a) (with the swap, load 0.90 to 1.02): M0 572.6, M1 677.8, M2 978.5, M3 1242.6 ms; the final state, twice (below): M0 518.7 and 530.7, M1 867.9 and 717.8, M3 963.4 and 1215.5 ms, against the baselines M0 636.6 and 562.1, M1 931.7 and 940.7, M3 1262.7 and 1459.2 ms. M2's own number is dominated by the swap (next entry). LCP stays between 0.43 and 0.73 s, CLS unchanged. The gain is modest, of the order of 50 to 250 ms, and inside the spread of single runs on M0; it is kept because every pair of runs on M1 and M3 moved the same way and it costs nothing measurable.
Tests that guard it: the whole browser suite (481 passed with it; the only failures were the two environment cases below and one timing flake that passed three times on rerun), `pages.spec.ts` geometry of beat 1, `nojs.spec.ts`, `a11y.spec.ts`, the M0 to M3 specs.

## 2026-10-10 P3-X1: remedy (b), KaTeX output, measured, route unchanged (request)
Decision: no change; request to the orchestrator with numbers. The route of decisions/P0-A3.md renders every formula as `htmlAndMathml`. In the built pages (before): M0 83 formulas, 1303 elements in `.katex-html` and 728 in `.katex-mathml` of 3222 elements, 58.3 kB and 21.0 kB of 232 kB; M1 955 and 570 of 6380 elements (43.2 kB and 17.4 kB of 564 kB); M2 1941 and 1123 of 5159 elements (87.2 kB and 32.6 kB of 337 kB); M3 951 and 526 of 6289 (43.0 kB and 16.4 kB of 514 kB). KaTeX's `output: 'mathml'` would drop the HTML layer (about 25 to 38 percent of a lesson's elements on M0 and M2) and keep MathML for assistive technology, so A-12 still holds; `output: 'html'` would drop MathML and break A-12, so it is not proposed. The cost: formulas would be drawn by the browser's MathML Core rendering with system fonts instead of KaTeX's layout, which looks different and needs the owner's eye in three engines (`mathml.spec.ts` checks MathML rendering, and WebKit and Firefox are not installed in this container), and the lesson tests that read `.katex` and `annotation` would have to be checked. Not worth changing the route for the lab timings alone.
