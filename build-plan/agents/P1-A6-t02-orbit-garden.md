## 1. Objective

When you are done the site has tool T2, the Orbit Garden (id `garden`, route `/tools/garden`): it grows a set of positions as a graph, one vertex per position found and one edge per move, coloured and labelled by node. In `roots` mode it grows the root populations from the single Martians; in `orbit` mode it grows the orbit of one position of either game. It grows by following the moves the reader makes on a board (`follow`) or by one breadth-first layer per press (`explore`), holds at most 2000 positions, says in the exact words of SPEC 4.0 and 5.5 which bound stopped it, offers the positive roots drawn in layers by total population with their raising moves, and counts what it has found against a total the reader reveals. M1 places it in beats 1, 4 and 5 (the triangle grown to 40 layers at build time is the module's cliffhanger); M3 (phase 2), M4 (phase 3) and M6 (phase 4) place it again, so every configuration they name must work through properties alone. Every acceptance criterion of SPEC 4 T2 except the phase-2 verdict becomes an automated test, which gate G1 requires.

### 5.3 What grows, and how (SPEC 4 T2 "What grows", "Modes of growth"; 5.4; 5.5; E-5, E-6)

- Verbatim: "**What grows.** In `roots` mode the seeds are all the single Martians and the moves are mutations, so the garden is the set of root populations. In `orbit` mode the seed is one position of either game (by default the current position of a T1 on the same page) and the garden is its orbit."
- `roots`: seeds $e_0,\dots,e_{n-1}$ in increasing position, the order of the oracle's `roots` (`[unit(n, i) for i in range(n)]`), game `'mutation'`. The garden is then the same set, in the same order, as `roots(board, CAPS.gardenPositions, maxDepth)`; test it.
- `orbit`: one seed in the board's arithmetic; the game is `'mutation'` for `coords: 'populations'` and `'firing'` for `coords: 'numbers'` (6.6: `coords` says "which game `start` is a position of"). The seed is, in order of precedence: the current position of the T1 named by `linkTo` (7.4; section 5.8); else the instance's `start`; else the default of 6.6 "all ones for numbers, one Martian in the first city for populations" (`defaultState`).
- The one call that grows the garden: `orbitGraph(board, seeds, game, CAPS.gardenPositions, maxDepth)`, never with `legalOnly` (the garden is the orbit under every move), never through the worker (5.4 below). Plan: recompute from the seeds on every growth step, so that the garden after $k$ layers is exactly the engine's result for `maxDepth` $k$; the order and truncation of 5.5 then hold by construction. A search of at most 2000 positions costs about 9 ms (5.5), so recomputing is cheap.
- `maxDepth`: in `explore`, the number of layers grown so far, never more than the layers bound; the layers bound is the `layers` property, default `CAPS.layersOffFinite` (40), on every board that is not of finite type (section 5.5) and absent on a named finite-type board (plan reading of 4.0 "default 40 on any board that `classify` does not call finite"; a page may still pass `layers`).
- Verbatim: "**Modes of growth**: `follow` (vertices appear as the reader visits them on the linked T1); `explore` (breadth-first, one layer per press of a button). The property `grown` gives the number of layers already grown when the page loads (default 0; M1 uses 40 on the triangle). That growth is computed at build time and is part of the default state."
- `explore`: one button (plan text `Grow one layer`, `data-control="grow"`) adds one layer per press. It is disabled when the search has completed (`truncated` false and `depthReached` less than `maxDepth`), when the layers bound is reached, and when the cap has cut the search. Nothing grows by itself (4.0: "Neither control acts by itself on page load"; A-9; P9). `grown` larger than the layers bound is clamped to it (plan).
- `follow` (plan mechanics, record them): the garden starts with the seed (the followed board's position when the garden attaches); every change of the followed state adds the new current position if it is new, with the edge from the previous position labelled by the node of the move just made; undo, redo, scrubbing and revisits add nothing. `grown` is ignored. In `roots` mode the followed board must stand on a root population (every position reached from one is one); in `orbit` mode the set is the orbit of the seed. A vertex keeps its place as others appear: rank it by its depth in the bounded `orbitGraph` of the followed start, computed once (plan).
- `grown` at build time: the component computes the garden synchronously in its script, so Astro's server render in Node writes it into the HTML (E-9: "At build time, when Astro renders the pages in Node, every call runs in-process"). Never compute the default growth in `$effect`, in the worker, or after hydration only.

## 9. Traps

1. "stopped after 40 layers" is said when `truncated` is false and `depthReached` equals `maxDepth`; "stopped after $N$ positions; layers 0 to $L$ are complete" when `truncated` is true (5.5). A search that completed (`depthReached` less than `maxDepth`) says nothing. `completeLayers` when the cap falls exactly at the end of a layer is still the depth being expanded (5.5; `P1-A1`'s trap); take it from the engine, never recompute it.
2. Edges are coloured by node AND labelled with the node label (6.3). Colour alone fails A-5. Label 0 (affine boards, phase 2) takes colour 8; labels 9 to 12 reuse colours 1 to 4.
3. The move graph has more edges than `orbitGraph` records (`parent` and `node` give one way in per position); apply every move once more to draw them all (5.4 note). Using `parent` alone draws a tree, not the garden.
4. Never say "not of finite type" from a cap; only `classify` may say it (4.0), and in phase 1 it does not exist. The stop message names the bound, nothing more.
5. Seed order: the oracle's `roots` seeds `[unit(n, i) for i in range(n)]`, the single Martians in increasing position, and `verify.py` seeds `layer_growth_examples` the same way. Another order changes `items`, the per-layer drawing order and the cap point at 2000 (which 2000 positions are kept), and counts by layer cannot detect it; compare against `roots(board, CAPS.gardenPositions)` in order.
6. `roots` against `orbit`: the orbit of $(1,0)$ on $B_2$ under mutation has 4 positions, while `roots` mode on $B_2$ finds 8 (the long and short roots lie in two orbits). M6 row 1 says "8 populations", so it needs `roots` mode grown in `explore` (`grown: 2`; section 5.1). `follow` cannot show 8 there even in `roots` mode: it adds only the positions the reader visits, and from $(1,0)$ the reader reaches only 4. `follow` on a populations T1 defaults to `grows: 'roots'` because of M1 row 1 on $A_2$, where the orbit of $(1,0)$ is all 6 root populations.
7. `fromAdjacency` takes $A$, not $C$; the triangle's `cartan` in `layer_growth_examples` is $C$. Use `resolveBoard({ cartan })` for a stored matrix.
8. Custom, stars and the triangle are `Board<bigint>` (E-2): `JSON.stringify` throws on them; key positions with `board.arith.show`; export through `exportJSON`.
