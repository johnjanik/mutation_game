## 5. Requirements

### 5.1 Conventions at your boundary

- `SPEC.md` section 2 is the single source of truth. $C_{ij}=\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle$; $A_{ij}$ is the number of roads from $i$ to $j$; $C=2I-A^{\mathsf T}$, "So $-C_{ij}$ is the number of roads from $j$ **into** $i$." Signatures, verbatim from `CONTRACTS.md` 2.4: `orbit<T>(board, seeds: Vec<T>[], game: Game, cap: number, maxDepth?: number): Enumeration<T[]>`; `orbitGraph<T>(board, seeds, game, cap, maxDepth?, legalOnly?: boolean): OrbitGraph<T>`; `wordTo(graph: OrbitGraph<unknown>, index: number): Word`; `roots(board, cap: number, maxDepth?: number): Enumeration<T[]>`; `positiveRoots(board, cap?, maxDepth?): T[][]` "sorted by total, then lexicographically".

`SPEC.md` 5.5, "Order and truncation of a search", the rule you implement, verbatim: "Positions are found in breadth-first order: the seeds in the order given, then, for each position in the order found, the nodes in increasing position. `items` is in that order, so the layers are contiguous. The last layer present is then incomplete, unless the cap fell exactly at the end of a layer. A tool says "stopped after $L$ layers" when `truncated` is false and `depthReached` equals `maxDepth` (the positions of that depth were not expanded, so more may exist), and "stopped after $N$ positions; layers 0 to $L$ are complete" when `truncated` is true. **`orbitGraph`** is the one search routine; `orbit`, `roots` and `positiveRoots` call it, and so will `legalLayers`, `orbitSize` checks and the tools of later phases. Port `orbit_graph_capped` (lines 1352 to 1370) with the `legal_only` test of `orbit_graph` (lines 1328 to 1349):

1. Key each position by "the entries joined as decimal text" (5.5, "Orbits"): `v.map(arith.show).join(',')`, in a `Map` from key to index in `items`.
2. Seeds, in the order given, copied, at depth 0 with `parent` and `node` `null`. A repeated seed is kept once, at its first occurrence (the oracle builds a dictionary).
3. Expand `items` in order (a read pointer over the array, no second queue): for the position `v` at index `h` with depth `d`, skip it when `maxDepth` is given and `d >= maxDepth`; otherwise for `i` from 0 to `n - 1`: when `legalOnly` is set and `arith.sign(v[i]) <= 0`, skip `i` ("follows only legal firings (a node with a positive number)", the oracle's docstring); let `w` be `mutate(board, v, i)` for `'mutation'` or `fire(board, v, i)` for `'firing'`; if `w` is new, then if `items.length === cap` return at once with `truncated: true` and `completeLayers: d`; otherwise append `w` with depth `d + 1`, `parent` `h` and `node` `i`.
4. When every item has been expanded or skipped: `truncated: false` and `completeLayers` equal to the largest depth present.

## 9. Traps

1. `highest_root_of_reversed_board` differs from the coroot of the highest root (roster). F11: "for $B_3$ the highest root of the reversed board is $(2,2,1)$, while $\theta^\vee=(1,2,1)$". Minuscule nodes use the reversed board, through `transposeBoard`.
2. `positive_roots` sorting: by total population first, then lexicographically as lists; match the oracle exactly (roster). The oracle's key is `(sum(r), r)`. Compare entries numerically, never as text: as strings `"10"` sorts before `"9"`, and `bigint` and `number` must not be compared with `<` across types.
3. `fired_tally` keys are strings in the golden file (roster): `{"1": 3, "2": 2, ...}`. Build the tally with `String(value)` keys; values are counts (numbers).
4. `coxeterNumber` $=2N/n$ needs the full root count; on $A_1$ it is 2 (roster). $N$ is the number of positive roots (1 on $A_1$), not the number of roots; a search capped below the root count gives a wrong $h$ silently, so use the internal cap of 5.8.
5. `orbitGraph`: `completeLayers` when the cap falls exactly at the end of a layer is still the depth of the position being expanded (roster; 5.5 "unless the cap fell exactly at the end of a layer. In both cases `completeLayers` is the depth of the position that was being expanded"). The triangle with a cap of 9 holds layers 0 and 1 whole and still reports 1, because the search stops on discovering the tenth position while expanding a position of depth 1.
6. On a one-way board everything in this phase must still work (`SPEC.md` 5.6 first row); do not guard here. Guards arrive in phase 2 (roster).
7. Positions against labels. The golden file holds labels; `longest_word`, `minus_w0` and `minuscule_nodes` are converted (rule 2); vectors (`positive_roots`, `highest_root`, the numbers, `highest_root_of_reversed_board`, the four `kostant` lists) are in label order and are not converted. `minus_w0` converts both the index (implicitly, by label order) and the value. On named boards labels are $1,\dots,n$, so a stray `+ 1` in `src/` passes the golden tests; the `subBoard` and `longestWord(board, nodes)` tests with labels other than $1,\dots,n$ catch it.
8. Rows against columns. Mutation reads row $i$ of $C$, firing reads column $i$; `mutationMatrix` replaces a row, `firingMatrix` a column. A transposed `cartan` passes every simply laced test and fails `B`, `C`, `F`, `G`.
9. `play` tests for the end before the cap: an empty `legalNodes` list gives `'ended'` even when `word.length === cap`. The oracle accepts a game of exactly `cap` moves and raises only on move `cap + 1`.
10. The oracle's `play_fate` (phase 2) places its cap test after recording the move, so its `cap`-th move reports `'cap'` even when the game ends there. That is `P2-A1`'s port; do not copy its order into `play`, `raisingPlay` or `kostantPlay`.
11. Python's `max` and `min` keep the first extreme element. `highestRoot` takes the first root of largest total in `positiveRoots` order and `highestShortRoot` the first of smallest total; on finite types there are no ties, but keep the order so that later fallbacks behave like the oracle.
12. The hash key is `arith.show` of each entry joined with commas. Do not key by `JSON.stringify` (it throws on `bigint`) or by the array object.
13. The oracle's `orbit` without `max_depth` returns an unordered set and raises at the cap. The engine keeps breadth-first order and reports `truncated`; never throw at a cap (E-10).
14. A position at depth `maxDepth` is not expanded, so a search ended by `maxDepth` has `truncated` false although more positions may exist. That is the case the tool reports as "stopped after $L$ layers" (5.5), not as truncation.
15. Speed: `Array.prototype.shift` on `items` makes the search quadratic; copying vectors that `mutate` already returned fresh doubles the memory. The star with five arms at 150000 positions is the test that notices.
16. `reference/bench_orbit.mjs` tests `row[j] !== 0` before multiplying. With a `bigint` matrix `0n !== 0` is true, so the test never skips; correct but slower, and a port that compares a `bigint` entry with a `number` elsewhere gets wrong answers. Compare with `arith.sign` or with the arithmetic's own zero.
17. `EngineOverflow` from `SafeInt` must pass through every function unchanged (E-10). A `try`/`catch` that converts it, or a fallback to `Big` inside the engine, breaks T1's reload rule (`SPEC.md` 4 T1). The engine never switches arithmetic by itself.
18. `bigint` division truncates toward zero and `%` keeps the sign of the dividend. Normalise every `Rational` with `gcd` of absolute values and move the sign to the numerator; `rat(1, -2)` must equal `rat(-1, 2)` under `ratEq` and print `"-1/2"`.
19. `coxeterNumber` uses integer division as the oracle does (`2 * N // n`). On the finite types it is exact; do not go through floating point, and do not use `Math.round`.
20. Fresh arrays everywhere (E-5). `fired` holds values copied out of the current vector; `position` is a new array; the seeds in `items` are copies. `p1-api.test.ts` freezes inputs to catch a write.
21. `raisingPlay` recomputes `raisingNodes` after every move, and the next move is the lowest raising position of the new population, not the next one in the old list. Kostant's game ends at the same population whatever the order (F13), but `moves_from_node` is only equal for every order because the length is also invariant; test both with `seededChoose`.
22. The finite functions on a one-way board: the plan's fallback of 5.8 throws `NotFiniteType` only when a search, a play or an assertion fails, which on a one-way board may or may not happen. `P2-A1`'s guard will throw `NeedsTwoWayRoads` there first. So no test of yours pins any outcome of a finite function on a one-way board.
23. `vitest` 5 and `fast-check` 4 postdate most training data (`COMMON.md` 3). Read the installed type definitions for `fc.assert` (options `seed`, `numRuns`), `fc.property`, `fc.integer`, `fc.array`, `fc.constantFrom`, and for Vitest's per-test timeout, before writing the forms from memory.
24. `determinant` is yours, but its golden key `types[*].det_cartan` belongs to `P2-A1` (a key is tested in exactly one file). Do not `covers()` it, read it or compare with it; test `determinant` with the table of 2.8 and with identities.
