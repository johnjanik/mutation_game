## 1. Objective

When you are done the site has tool T13 Satake Studio (id `satake`, route `/tools/satake`, embed route `/embed/satake`): the reader paints the nodes of a finite-type board black or white on a copy of the diagram drawn directly above the game board, picks $\tau$ among the symmetries of order at most 2, and sees three lights (Kolb's three conditions, each judged on its own, each red light with its reason in words written from the engine's `Reason` records with node labels). When the pair is not admissible no data are shown and the careful sentence of SPEC 2.15 explains what that means; when it is admissible the tool shows the name or names of the real form and its data, plays $\theta$ on a chosen root in three stages, draws the shadows (a picture for real rank 1 and 2, a table otherwise), draws the restricted board in Bourbaki order with each click playing its composite word step by step on the big board, lists the catalogue of the board, and exports the diagram as `\dynkin` LaTeX. Lesson M9 (`P6-A7`, stage 6.3) places the tool in eight of its nine beats through the presets of CONTRACTS 6. Every acceptance criterion of SPEC 4 T13 is an automated test, which gate G6 checks under "acceptance of T13 and T14 as automated tests", and `T13-keyboard.spec.ts` is the T13 keyboard script of the gate item "keyboard scripts for T1, T4, T13".

## 9. Traps

- The decoration never competes with the sign encoding: it sits on its own copy of the diagram above the board, nodes aligned (6.3, roster). Black is never the fill of a game-board node.
- The restricted board's nodes are drawn in Bourbaki order (`restricted_orbits_in_bourbaki_order`), which may differ from the computed orbit order (roster). `rb.orbits`, `rb.compositeWords`, `restricted_simple_orbits`, `restricted_simple_multiplicities` and `restricted_cartan` are in orbit order (least node first); on `e6(-14)` the orbit order is `[[1, 6], [2]]` and the Bourbaki order `[[2], [1, 6]]`. Clicking drawn node $k$ must play the word of the orbit drawn there, found by orbit, not by index.
- The `C2` adjustment is `satakeRecord`'s, not `satake`'s: `satake(d)` says `B2` with the long node first; the four `C2` records reverse the orbits. Applying it twice, or to a `B2` record such as `so(2,6)`, swaps the long and short nodes on the picture while every number stays right.
- Shadows are kept as `2 alpha-bar` integers (`shadow2`); draw at half (roster). A.5 prints halves; the engine returns doubles. Never halve inside an engine call, and never use floats for anything but the picture's coordinates.
- Never print "theta is not an involution" for a failed condition 3 (M9 beat 4, roster). On $A_2$ with node 1 black, $\theta=-s_1$ is an involution that permutes the roots; what fails is that no involution of the Lie algebra matches it.

- $\tau$ may move black nodes and may be non-trivial with no arrow drawn: `so(p, 2n-p)` with $p\le n-2$ and $n-p$ odd, and the compact forms of $A_n$ ($n\ge2$), $D_n$ ($n$ odd) and $E_6$ (2.15).
