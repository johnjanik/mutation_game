## 9. Traps

1. Moods are the signs of $a_i=(Cp)_i$: unhappy is negative (a mutation would raise), manic is positive (it would lower) (SPEC 2.16, roster). D7: the number is the drop ($a=Cp$), not Wildberger's gain $-Cp$ of 2.4(e); flipping the sign swaps unhappy and manic.
2. Raising mode allows a city whose number is negative; legal mode (T1, numbers) allows a node whose number is positive. Do not reuse T1's legal test.
3. On $B$, $C$, $F$, $G$ the end depends on whether the start is long or short (F13, roster). Phase 1 has no symmetrizer: decide the name by comparing the end with `highestRoot` and `highestShortRoot`, not by guessing lengths from labels. Which city is long differs between the two numberings of the rank 2 board: on `B2` city 1 ends at (1, 2), the highest root; on `C2` city 1 ends at (1, 1), the highest short root (`kostant.B2`, `kostant.C2`; D19). On `C3` city 3 is long; on `G2` city 1 is short and city 2 long.
4. $h-2$ holds only on simply laced boards. `B2` ends after 1 move (a rise of 2 at once) while $h-2=2$; `G2` ends after 2 while $h-2=4$. Never show $h-2$ there.

6. The return counts are not the final numbers off the simply laced boards ($B_2$ city 1: $a=(0,2)$, $b=(0,1)$). The recipe sends $a_j$ roads from the new city to city $j$ and receives $b_j$ back; in matrix terms column 0 holds $-a_j$ and row 0 holds $-b_j$. Do not transpose them.

8. The oracle's `raising_play` picks `unhappy[0]`, recomputes the numbers after every move, and raises `OverflowError` after `cap + 1` moves (default 100000). The engine reports `status: 'cap'` with exactly `cap` moves and its default cap is `CAPS.playMoves`, 20000 (`P1-A1`). No finite play comes near it; never pass a larger cap.
9. The golden ends and lengths were generated with random choices (`verify.py` lines 876 to 881); the lowest-position play and any hand-chosen order give the same results on finite types. A test that pins the word must use the lowest-position rule (A.4 lists those words).
