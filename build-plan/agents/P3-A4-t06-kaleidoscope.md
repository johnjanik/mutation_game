## 1. Objective

When you are done the site has tool T6, the Kaleidoscope (id `mirror`, route `/tools/mirror`): for the rank 2 boards $A_2$, $B_2$, $C_2$, $G_2$ it draws the plane with the two mirrors, the simple roots as the mirror normals in the triangular basis of `choleskyBasis`, the chambers, and one point of the weight lattice. The point is a position $a$ of the numbers game; its numbers are drawn as scaled distances to the mirrors (SPEC 2.4(d)) and its population $p=C^{-1}a$ as a parallelogram along the normals and as exact fractions. Firing a node reflects the point and updates both readouts and the line $a=Cp$; the point is placed by buttons and keys as well as by dragging with snapping, and three overlays (all roots, the orbit, the two lattices) can be switched on. M4 places it in beat 4 (on $A_2$ at $a=(1,1)$) and M2 beat 4 is to show it in place of a static picture. Every acceptance criterion of SPEC 4 T6 becomes an automated test, which gate G3 requires.

## 9. Traps

- Roster: "The number on node i is 2/|alpha_i| times the signed distance to mirror i (2.4(d)); the scaling differs between long and short roots." The drawn distance is $a_i|\alpha_i|/2$: on $B_2$ node 1 (long, $|\alpha_1|=2$) the distance equals $a_1$; node 2 (short, $\sqrt2$) gives $a_2/\sqrt2$. Equal numbers do not mean equal distances.
- Roster: "B2 and C2 are the same board with nodes exchanged; offer both names, draw each in its own numbering (D19)." `C2` is not `B2` relabelled on screen: $\alpha_1$ is short on `C2` and long on `B2`, and the angle is 135 degrees on both.
- `choleskyBasis` returns `U` as a list of **rows**, and the picture of $\alpha_j$ is **column** $j$ (oracle docstring). Reading row 1 as $\alpha_2$ gives $(0, U_{11})$, a root at 90 degrees: the transposition trap of this tool. The unit test on the drawn vectors catches it.
- $B=DC$, rows scaled: $B_{ij}=d_iC_{ij}$ (oracle `gram`). With $C^{\mathsf T}$ in place of $C$ (Bourbaki's matrix) the symmetrizer is reversed and $B_2$ is drawn as $C_2$. Compare with the golden `symmetrizer` ($B_2$ `[2, 1]`, $C_2$ `[1, 2]`).
- The point is $U\,C^{-1}a$, not $Ua$ (which would treat the numbers as root coordinates) and not $U^{\mathsf T}$ anything. $C^{-1}a$ is `toPopulation`, which is the oracle's `weight_to_root_coords` (`solve(C, a)`), not $C^{-\mathsf T}a$.
- `fire` uses **column** $i$ of $C$ ($a_j-C_{ji}a_i$, oracle `fire`: `a[j] - C[j][i] * ai`); the population changes in entry $i$ only, by $-a_i$ (the mutation), not by $-a$. A symmetric board ($A_2$) hides both errors; $B_2$, $C_2$, $G_2$ show them, so test all four.
- The site's sign is $a=Cp$, "the drop" (2.4(a), D7); 2.4(e): Wildberger's dual population is $q=-Cp$, "the gain". The bridge sentence says "lower"; never invert it.
- SVG's $y$ axis points down: without the flip $\alpha_2$ is drawn clockwise, at $-135$ degrees on $B_2$. 4 T6 says "counterclockwise".
- Snapping in a skew lattice: rounding $a_1$ and $a_2$ separately can pick a farther point when the basis angle is 120 or 150 degrees; search a neighbourhood (5.6).
- Floats in markup: the server render and the hydrated render must agree; format plane coordinates to fixed decimals and never let $-0$ appear as `-0.000` (plan: normalise to `0.000`).
- `toPopulation` returns `Rational` with `bigint` fields: `ratToString` gives `"2/3"`, `"-1/3"`, `"2"`; `data-value` uses that text; the stacked fraction on screen uses U+2212. On $G_2$ every population is an integer (no fraction bar); on $A_2$ thirds, on $B_2$ and $C_2$ halves.
- On $G_2$ the root lattice and the weight lattice are the same set ($\det C=1$); two overlapping patterns that hide each other read as a missing pattern. Draw both markers.
- A node whose number is 0 is on its mirror: firing it leaves the point and both readouts unchanged but adds a move (free play). Do not disable the button (7.5 A-3: "Node 5, value 0, cannot be fired" applies "in legal play; in free play every node can be fired"; T6 plays freely, 2.3).
