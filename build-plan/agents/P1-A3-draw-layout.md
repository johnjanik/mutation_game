### 5.1 Coordinates, labels and kinds (6.1; E-7)

- The table of 6.1 speaks of **labels** ("node $i$"). A board carries `labels` per position (E-7): $1..n$ on named finite boards and on `pentagon`, `square` and custom boards; $0,1,..,n$ on untwisted affine boards (the added node first) and on twisted boards ("in the order of its matrix in `twisted_affine`"). Look the label up in `board.labels[k]` for position `k`; never compute a label as position plus one or a position as label minus one. Return coordinates indexed by **position**, because `Board.svelte` and `Roads.svelte` index by position.
- Grid coordinates have $y$ pointing **up**: $D_n$ puts node $n-1$ at $(n-1,\,0.6)$ and node $n$ at $(n-1,\,-0.6)$, and "node 2 at $(3,1)$, above node 4". SVG's $y$ points down; the flip happens once, in `geometry.ts` (section 5.4).
- Signature (plan): `nodePositions(board: Pick<AnyBoard, 'n' | 'labels' | 'name' | 'cartan' | 'kind'>, xy?: readonly (readonly [number, number])[]): DrawLayout`, where `DrawLayout` (plan) extends `P0-A5`'s `GridLayout` with `circle: boolean` (true when the nodes sit on a circle and the polygon radius applies) and keeps `xy` (by position), `columns` and `kind`. Taking a structural `Pick` lets the tests pass plain objects for the boards that `boardFromName` cannot yet build.
- `kind` (consumed by `arrowTarget`, A-1): `'row'` for every row-with-branches layout of the table (all finite types, every untwisted affine board except $\tilde A_n$ with $n\ge2$, the twisted boards); `'polygon'` for $\tilde A_n$ with $n\ge2$, `pentagon` and `square`; `'custom'` for every custom board, whether placed at its `xy` or on the circle. A-1, verbatim: "On polygon layouts and on custom boards the arrows follow the labels and wrap round: Right and Down to the next label (clockwise on a polygon), Left and Up to the previous one." $\tilde A_1$ is `'row'` (plan; record it).
- `columns` is "the number of grid columns the board occupies (for a row of nodes, the number of nodes)": max $x$ minus min $x$ plus 1 for row and grid-placed custom layouts. It drives $u$. $E_8$ occupies 7 columns, $\tilde E_8$ 8, $\tilde C_8$ 9, $D_n$ and $\tilde D_n$ $n-1$, $\tilde B_n$ $n$, $\tilde C_n$ $n+1$, $\tilde G_2$ 3. For circle layouts `columns` is not used by the size rule (section 5.4).

### 5.4 Size on screen and hit areas (6.1; A-7)

- Verbatim, 6.1: "One grid unit is $u=\min(64,\lfloor (W-16)/k\rfloor)$ CSS pixels, where $W$ is the width of the board's box and $k$ the number of grid columns the board occupies (for a row of nodes, the number of nodes). On a viewport narrower than 400 pixels the box runs from edge to edge of the viewport, so $W$ is the viewport width. The hit area of a node is a square of side $\min(44,u)$ centred on it; with the layouts above no two hit areas overlap. A polygon layout is inscribed in a circle of radius $(\min(W,360)-60)/2$, which keeps the corners of a polygon with up to twelve nodes far enough apart for hit areas of 44."
- `geometry.ts` exports (plan names): `gridUnit(W: number, k: number): number` = `Math.min(64, Math.floor((W - 16) / k))`; `hitSide(u: number): number` = `Math.min(44, u)`; `polygonRadius(W: number): number` = `(Math.min(W, 360) - 60) / 2`; `boardBoxWidth(viewport: number, container: number): number` returning `viewport` when `viewport < 400`, else `container`; `toPixels(layout: DrawLayout, W: number): { centres: [number, number][]; hit: number; width: number; height: number }` that maps row and grid-placed layouts with $u$ (8 pixels of margin on each side, matching the 16 of the formula; plan), maps circle layouts with `polygonRadius` and a hit side of 44, and flips $y$ for SVG. The width and height let a tool reserve its box in the server HTML (7.6: "Tools reserve their box size in the HTML so that hydration does not shift the page").
- The hit square of side `hit` that `toPixels` returns is the transparent hit area of A-7 ("by a transparent hit area when the drawn node is smaller"): the tool draws it as a transparent square centred on the node, around a drawn node that may be smaller, and it is that square, not the drawn circle, that takes the pointer. `Board.svelte` draws it (`P0-A5`'s, extended by `P1-A5` in stage 1.2); you supply its side and centre.
- On a circle layout the hit side is 44: A-7, "Polygon layouts always reach 44". The circle fallback of custom boards uses the same radius (plan reading: it is a polygon layout in all but name; record it).
- A-7, verbatim: "Targets are 44 by 44 CSS pixels where the layout allows (beyond AA, by choice) and never less than 24 by 24 with no overlap (the AA requirement), by a transparent hit area when the drawn node is smaller. The rule is in section 6.1: the hit area of a node is a square of side $\min(44,u)$ pixels, $u$ being the grid unit. In a box 320 pixels wide this gives 44 for boards with at most six nodes in a row, 43 for seven ($E_8$), 38 for eight, 33 for nine (the affine board of $C_8$) and 25 for twelve (the largest custom board). Polygon layouts always reach 44."
- The numbers your tests must reproduce (verify.py section 20): `min(44, gridUnit(320, k))` for $k=6,7,8,9,12$ is `[44, 43, 38, 33, 25]`; for every $k$ from 1 to 12 it is at least 24 (the full list at 320 is 44, 44, 44, 44, 44, 44, 43, 38, 33, 30, 27, 25); `min(44, gridUnit(360, 7))` is 44; at $W=320$ the radius is 130, and for every polygon of $m=3..12$ corners `2 * 130 * sin(pi / m) >= 44 * sqrt(2)` (the smallest, $m=12$, is 67.29 against 62.23), and `2 * 130 + 44 <= 320 - 16`.
- 4.0, "Small screens": "Every tool is usable at a width of 320 CSS pixels. Targets are 44 pixels where the layout allows and never below 24; the rule is in section 6.1, and at that width boards with more than six nodes in one row get less than 44."
- `P0-A5`'s `board/layout.ts` already has `gridUnit` and `hitSide` for the row families. Yours are the full versions; do not edit `board/layout.ts` (not yours). Ask `P1-A5`, who extends `site/src/components/board/*` in stage 1.2, to switch `Board.svelte` to `nodePositions` and `toPixels`, to draw each node's transparent hit area as the square of side `hit` from `toPixels` centred on the node, and to apply `boardBoxWidth` (`requests`).

### 5.5 Roads (6.2; 2.1)

- Verbatim, 6.2: "`dynkin` (default for bidirected boards): one, two or three parallel lines between joined nodes; when the two counts differ, a chevron on the lines pointing in the direction of the extra roads, that is, from the long node to the short node. Pairs with $A_{ij}A_{ji}\ge4$ (only on custom and affine boards) are drawn as a thick line with the two counts written at its ends." and "`roads`: every road is drawn as its own curved arrow, as in the Mars and Venus pictures. Used in M1, M4 and M6 where the counts in each direction are the subject, and **always** on a board with a one-way road, where the `dynkin` style would be ambiguous."
- Road counts come from the engine: `A = adjacency(board)`, `A[i][j]` = roads from position $i$ to position $j$ $=-C_{ji}$. Never read them off `C[i][j]` directly (that is roads from $j$ into $i$; 2.1: "$-C_{ij}$ is the number of roads from $j$ **into** $i$"). Two nodes are joined when `A[i][j] > 0` or `A[j][i] > 0`.
- `effectiveRoadStyle(board, requested?: 'dynkin' | 'roads'): 'dynkin' | 'roads'` (plan name): `'roads'` whenever `board.kind === 'one-way'` (equivalently `boardKind(board.cartan) === 'one-way'`), whatever was requested; otherwise `requested ?? 'dynkin'`.
- `roadGlyphs(board, style)` (plan name) returns, by position pair $i<j$ that is joined, in increasing $(i,j)$ order (deterministic): in the `dynkin` style with $m=A_{ij}$, $m'=A_{ji}$: if $mm'\le3$, `{ kind: 'lines', from: i, to: j, count: max(m, m'), chevronTowards: m === m' ? null : (m > m' ? j : i) }`; if $mm'\ge4$, `{ kind: 'thick', from: i, to: j, countAtFrom, countAtTo }`. In the `roads` style, one `{ kind: 'arrow', from, to, index, of }` per road in each direction, with distinct curvatures so that every arrow is visible (up to 4 each way on editor boards, 5.6; any count from the engine).
- Direction checks your tests must hold (from the oracle's `cartan`, 2.1 and 2.9): on $B_n$, $A_{n-1,n}=2$: two lines, chevron towards node $n$ (the short node); on $C_n$ the chevron points towards node $n-1$; on $F_4$ two lines between nodes 2 and 3, chevron towards node 3; on $G_2$ three lines, chevron towards node 1 ($\alpha_1$ short). On a simply laced board every glyph is one line with no chevron.
- The thick line's "two counts written at its ends" does not say which count stands at which end. Plan: write at each end the number of roads leaving that node (`countAtFrom` = $A_{ij}$ beside node $i$); record it. Examples: $\tilde A_1$ (`[[2,-2],[-2,2]]`) gives 2 and 2; `A2^(2)` (`[[2,-4],[-1,2]]`) gives 1 beside node 0 and 4 beside node 1.
- `Roads.svelte`: rewrite the internals to draw these glyphs with stroke `var(--road)` and arrowheads and chevrons in `var(--arrow)`; props unchanged (`board`, `xy`, `unit`, `roadStyle`, as `P0-A5` defined them). The counts on a thick line are SVG text in `var(--text)`. The drawing stays `aria-hidden` as `P0-A5` built it; the buttons carry the semantics. A-6: "outlines, roads, arrows and focus rings at least 3 to 1".
- So that the colours `Roads.svelte` uses are testable without a DOM (plan; record it): `roads.ts` also exports `roadElements(glyphs, centres)` returning plain SVG element descriptors (`{ tag: 'line' | 'path' | 'polygon' | 'text'; attrs: Record<string, string>; text?: string }`) whose `stroke` and `fill` are the strings `var(--road)`, `var(--arrow)` and `var(--text)`; `Roads.svelte` renders those descriptors and adds nothing of its own except the `aria-hidden` wrapper.

### 5.8 The layered layout (7.1; A-11; 7.4; E-9)

- 7.1, verbatim: "a layout library for graphs, because every graph the site draws is ranked (by number of moves or by height) and a purpose-built layered layout is simpler and deterministic. If a general layout is ever needed, use `d3-dag` 1.2.2 or `@dagrejs/dagre` 3.1.1." Add neither; no dependency at all.
- Signature (plan): `layeredLayout(input: { keys: readonly string[]; rank: readonly number[]; edges: readonly (readonly [number, number])[] }): { x: number[]; y: number[]; order: number[][]; widestRank: number; rankCount: number }`. Vertices are indices into `keys`; ranks are non-negative integers given by the caller (the depth of `orbitGraph`, the total population of a root, the length of a Weyl group element).
- Rules: `y[v] = rank[v]` (ranks kept exactly). Rank order: the lowest rank sorted by key; each higher rank sorted by the barycenter (mean `x`-index) of its neighbours in all lower ranks, ties and vertices with no lower neighbour broken by key (plan: plain code-point string comparison, never `localeCompare`; record it). Edges may join ranks that are not adjacent (T2's rises of 2 and 3 on $B$, $C$, $F$, $G$ span two or three layers) and must be accepted. Coordinates are integers: `x[v]` is the index in its rank plus `Math.floor((widestRank - size) / 2)` to centre the rank (plan). `widestRank` is the size of the largest rank, "so the tool can scroll inside its box per A-11".
- Deterministic: the result depends only on the multiset of (key, rank) and the edge set, not on the order of `keys` or `edges` in the input (keys are distinct; throw a `RangeError` on a duplicate key, plan). Server render and hydrated render must agree; the same code renders T8's SVG snapshot in Node (7.4: "the layered layout for T8"). DOM-free.
- A-11, verbatim: "Prose and controls reflow at 320 CSS pixels. A diagram that cannot (a wide orbit graph, the Weyl Explorer) scrolls inside its own box, which is the exception the criterion allows for two-dimensional content."
- Main thread (7.6, E-9): it runs on the main thread for at most 2000 vertices (T2's bound) and 1152 group elements (T8). Sort once per rank; no quadratic pass over all vertex pairs. Time it once on 2000 vertices in your test run and write the figure in the fragment; do not assert a time.
