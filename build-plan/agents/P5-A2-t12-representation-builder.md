### 5.3 The orbit drawn in layers (T12 bullet 1; 6.3; A-5; 5.4 note on move graphs)

- Quoted: "the orbit drawn in layers with edges coloured by node".
- Layers (plan): `orbitGraph(board, [omega_i], 'firing', CAPS.repOrbit)`; the layer of a position is its `depth` (layer 0 is $\omega_i$ alone). Edges (5.4: "A tool that draws all of them applies every move to every position of `items` once more"): one undirected edge between $\mu$ and $R_k\mu$ for every position $\mu$ and node $k$ with $\mu_k\ne0$; a node with $\mu_k=0$ fixes $\mu$ and draws nothing. On a minuscule orbit every edge joins adjacent layers; the end nearer $\omega_i$ (smaller depth, the higher weight, drawn higher with layer 0 at the top) has $\mu_k=1$, and the edge is the entry of $F_k$ from that end to $R_k\mu$, whose $k$-th number is $-1$ (the deeper end, the lower weight; oracle on $A_2$ node 1: the edge from `(1,0)` at layer 0 to `(-1,1)` is `F_1 = {2,0: 1}`); on a non-minuscule orbit the edges where $\mu_k=\pm2$ have no entry in $E_k$ or $F_k$, which is the failure made visible (plan: draw those edges dashed, with a legend sentence).

Print the node label on or beside each edge. Each vertex shows its position as text (with true minus signs) and its basis index (plan).
- Drawing bound (plan, borrowed from T2's 300 of 5.6): draw every vertex and edge up to 300 positions; above 300 (non-minuscule orbits only) draw the layer sizes as a column of counts with one sentence saying the full graph is not drawn. Say this in words (4.0 Stated limits). Record the number.

### 5.6 The relation checker (T12 bullet 3; 2.18; 5.2; 6.6 `q`; 7.3)

### 5.7 Failure for nodes that are not minuscule (T12 acceptance; 2.18; F11)

- Quoted (T12 acceptance): "choosing the non-minuscule node 2 of $D_4$ shows a failing relation and the explanation that some number in the orbit is 2."
- The explanation, `data-out="explanation"`, is computed by the engine (plan, from F11's third condition, which needs no enumeration and works above the cap): run `play(board, omega_i)` (legal play, lowest position first, at most $N\le120$ moves) and take the first fired number greater than 1. The fired number is the number on that node in a position of the orbit. ## 9. Traps

- **The basis does not start at $\omega_i$.** Decreasing lexicographic order puts the largest tuple first, which is often not the highest weight: $A_3$ node 2 starts `(1,0,-1)`, $E_6$ node 1 starts `(1,1,0,-1,0,0)`, $D_4$ node 2 starts `(2,-1,0,0)`. Layer 0 of the drawing is $\omega_i$; row 1 of a matrix is `basis[0]`. Never assume they coincide. (Oracle `sorted(..., reverse=True)`; E-5.)
- **Row is the image, column the source.** The oracle writes `(idx[b], idx[a])` for $a\mapsto b$. A grid drawn transposed makes $E_k$ look like $F_k$ while the engine's relation results stay correct, so nothing on screen contradicts it; only tests on the worked entries of 5.4 and 5.8 catch it.
- **Firing, not mutation.** The representation lives on positions of the numbers game (weights); every move is `fire` ($a_j\leftarrow a_j-C_{ji}a_i$), and a single step subtracts column $i$ of $C$ (2.18). Using `mutate` or a row of $C$ gives right answers on simply laced boards and wrong ones on $B_n$, $C_n$, $F_4$, $G_2$; test `B3` node 3 and `C3` node 1.
- **`chevalleySerre` never throws on failure.** It returns both sides for every relation, so the tool can show the failure (roster). Look relations up by `relation`, `i`, `j`, never by index into the list.
- **Positions against labels (E-7).** `RelationCheck.i` and `.j` are positions; `q`, the formula subscripts and `data-*` are labels. The relation code's labels go through `toPositions`; the 18 golden `node` values are labels.
- **Non-minuscule entries of 2.** On a non-minuscule orbit the oracle leaves $E_k$ and $F_k$ empty where $\mu_k=\pm2$ but $H_k$ still carries the 2 and the move matrix still moves the position. Do not "repair" this in the tool; it is the failure the reader is meant to see.
- **`minusculeMatrices` is an enumeration (E-9; roster).** The roster's trap reads: "minusculeMatrices is listed in E-9 as an enumeration: above 2000 expected items it goes to the worker; the orbit sizes are known from orbitSize beforehand." SPEC T12 settles the case above 2000 differently, and this file follows SPEC (plan reading; list it under `deviations` as superseding the roster's wording): "For a node that is not minuscule the matrices are built only when the orbit of $\omega_i$ has at most 2000 positions (`orbitSize`; node 2 of $D_4$ has 24), and otherwise the tool shows the explanation alone." So above 2000 the tool does not call `minusculeMatrices` at all, in the worker or elsewhere; every minuscule orbit has at most 256 positions, so no orbit the tool builds has more than 2000 positions. What remains of the roster's trap applies to every call at or below 2000: it is an enumeration, its size is known from `orbitSize` beforehand, and it is routed through `whereToRun` with that size (section 5.12). Decide with `orbitSize` before calling; pass `CAPS.repOrbit`. `orbitSize` is phase 3 and takes a dominant position; $\omega_i$ is dominant. Record this reading in your fragment.
- **Grids up to 27 inclusive, lists above (5.6; roster).** The 27 of $E_6$ is a grid; the 56 of $E_7$ is a list.
- **The string viewer's $\mu$ is not the representation.** Its line may leave the orbit (beat 1: `(0,1)` is outside the orbit of `(2,0)`); membership is about the orbit of $\mu$, not of $\omega_i$. And `stringThrough` asserts $\mu_i\ge0$ in the oracle: never call it with a negative entry at $i$ (5.9).
- **B2 and C2 are two picker entries (D19), with transposed matrices.** The golden records `B2` node 2 and `C2` node 1 are the same 4-dimensional representation seen from the two numberings.
- **Island props are serialised.** Pass board names and labels, not `Board` objects or `Map`s; rebuild inside the island. `SparseMatrix` values are `bigint`: convert for display and for `exportJSON` (decimal strings when unsafe, numbers when safe).
- **MathML inside KaTeX classes disappears** (KaTeX's stylesheet hides `.katex-mathml`, `P3-A5`'s trap): keep relation formulas outside any `.katex` element. Never import KaTeX into a `.svelte` file.
