Beat by beat, the ideas each paragraph must carry (SPEC 3 M8 and 2.18):

1. **Strings.** At a position, the number on node $i$ says how the position sits in a line of positions spaced by $\alpha_i$; firing node $i$ flips the line end over end. In Dynkin labels a step subtracts or adds column $i$ of $C$. The viewer shows $\mu,\ \mu-\alpha_i,\ \dots,\ \mu-\mu_i\alpha_i$ for $\mu_i\ge0$, and accepts any position, so it can show three or more positions, which a minuscule orbit never has (4 T12). General statement: in any finite-dimensional representation the weights through $\mu$ in direction $\alpha_i$ form an unbroken string $\mu-r\alpha_i,\dots,\mu+q\alpha_i$ with $r-q=a_i$. Provenance: standard; [Hum72] sections 9.4 and 21.3 only inside `<Unverified item="E10">` (5.7).
2. **Single steps and the counting operator.** $F_i$ and $E_i$ are single steps down and up the string; $H_i$ multiplies a position by its number $\mu_i$. The smallest case is $A_1$ with numbers $1$ and $-1$ (the two positions through `<G>`, 5.8).
3. **The $3\times3$ matrices of $A_2$.** The three positions of $A_2$ from $(1,0)$ (count, size and positions through `<G>` or `{{path}}`, 5.8); the recipe of 2.18 on a position $\mu$: "$H_k$ multiplies by the number $\mu_k$; $F_k$ sends $\mu$ to the fired position $R_k\mu$ when $\mu_k=1$, and to 0 otherwise; $E_k$ sends $\mu$ to $R_k\mu$ when $\mu_k=-1$, and to 0 otherwise." The reader checks $[E_1,F_1]=H_1$ and $[E_1,F_2]=0$ by clicking (T12). State the Chevalley-Serre relations here or in beat 4 exactly as 2.18 writes them, through `<Tex>` (or `$$` if route (a) is recorded): `[H_i,H_j]=0,\quad [H_i,E_j]=C_{ij}E_j,\quad [H_i,F_j]=-C_{ij}F_j,\quad [E_i,F_j]=\delta_{ij}H_i` and `\operatorname{ad}(E_i)^{1-C_{ij}}E_j=0,\qquad \operatorname{ad}(F_i)^{1-C_{ij}}F_j=0\quad(i\ne j)`, with $C_{ij}=\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle$ (the site's convention; say once that Bourbaki prints the transpose if you quote a source).
4. **The same recipe on bigger orbits.** The 8 positions of $D_4$ from node 1, the 27 of $E_6$ and the 56 of $E_7$; all the defining relations hold [V]. The construction works exactly when the node is minuscule (F11: every number in the orbit of $\omega_i$ is $-1$, $0$ or $1$; their number is $\det C-1$). For a node that is not minuscule the recipe fails: checked on $\omega_2$ of $D_4$ [V], whose orbit has `types.D4.fundamental_orbit_sizes[1]` (24) positions and contains a number 2. Say which representations were checked, exactly: the 18 of 2.18 ("five minuscule nodes of $A_1$ to $A_4$; the spin representations of $B_2$, $B_3$, $B_4$; the vector representations of $C_2$, $C_3$; all three minuscule nodes of $D_4$; nodes 1 and 5 of $D_5$; the two 27s of $E_6$ and the 56 of $E_7$"), never "every minuscule representation".
5. **A move as a group element.** The firing move is the permutation matrix $E_k+F_k+P_k^0$, $P_k^0$ the projection onto positions with $\mu_k=0$. The group element $\exp(E_k)\exp(-F_k)\exp(E_k)$ equals $E_k-F_k+P_k^0$, the same matrix with one sign changed in each pair, and its square is $(-1)^{H_k}$ [V]. "So in a minuscule representation a game move is a Lie group element with its signs removed" (2.18; in your own words). This answers the module's question: half a move is a single step.
6. **Why $E_8$ is different.** $E_8$, $F_4$ and $G_2$ have no minuscule node (`types.E8.minuscule_nodes` with `as="count"` gives 0; equivalently $\det C=1$, `types.E8.det_cartan`), which is why $E_8$ is the case Wildberger's method leaves out ([W03a]). "The site shows their weights (for the adjoint: roots and zeros) and says plainly that the matrices need more than the game [D]": say it plainly.

## 9. Traps

- **Two meanings of "mutation"** (roster). Fomin-Zelevinsky mutation changes the quiver; Wildberger's never changes the board. Never write that a mutation "changes the graph", even in passing.
- **Two meanings of "arrow"** (roster; 2.16; SOURCES 11.3). A quiver arrow is an orientation and gives a symmetric Cartan matrix; Wildberger's unequal road counts (and the Dynkin arrow) mark long and short. Do not draw a quiver arrow and a road-count arrow the same way.
- **Wildberger's Theorem 4.1 is stated without proof; the proofs are in [Gre13]** (roster; 2.18; SOURCES 11.1). Never cite [W03a] for a proof, and present the relations on the page as checked by computation for 18 representations.
- **[Hum72] was never opened**: every section number is [U] (E10). Cite the book bare, or wrap the numbers.
- **The 18 representations are not all minuscule representations**: $D_5$ node 4, nodes 1 and 3 of $A_3$ and others are minuscule but not in the list. Say "for 18 representations" and list them as 2.18 does.
- **Basis order and matrix orientation**: the basis is the orbit in decreasing lexicographic order of positions (E-5; the oracle sorts with `reverse=True`), so for $A_2$ node 1 it is $(1,0)$, $(0,-1)$, $(-1,1)$, not the order of play. An entry `(row, column)` has the image in the row and the source in the column. A sentence that names "row 3" must match the tool.
- **$F$ goes down, $E$ goes up**: $F_k$ acts when $\mu_k=1$ (the fired position is lower), $E_k$ when $\mu_k=-1$. A single step down subtracts column $k$ of $C$ (not row $k$; $C_{ij}=\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle$, Kac's convention).
- **The viewer's line is not the whole string**: `stringThrough` gives $\mu,\dots,\mu-\mu_i\alpha_i$, the part that firing turns end over end; the string of a representation is $\mu-r\alpha_i,\dots,\mu+q\alpha_i$ with $r-q=a_i$. Keep the two statements distinct.
- **Moves in the representation are firings on positions (weights)**, not mutations of populations. M8 is in the Numbers view: "node", not "city", in beats 1 to 6.
- **The signed move is $E_k-F_k+P_k^0$**, not $E_k+F_k-P_k^0$; its square is $(-1)^{H_k}$, not the identity.
- **Grid or list**: dimension at most 27 is a grid; above, a list. $E_6$ (27) is a grid; $E_7$ (56) is a list. Do not caption the 56 as a grid.
