### 5.3 Beat 2: the builder on $A_2$, the recipe is forced

- Idea (beat 2, verbatim): "The reader builds the form on a new board by adjusting weights until the meter holds still, and so discovers that the recipe is forced."
- Figure (table row 2, verbatim): "T3 builder on $A_2$: three sliders (two city weights, one road weight); passes exactly when all three are equal". Concretely: `b3` T3 with `preset="builder"`, `board="A2"`, `client:visible`; leave the initial weights at the preset's default or pass a failing start (for example city weights 2 and 2, road weight 1) through the prop `P2-A2` named, so that the reader has something to fix [D]; record the choice. Note that the start must not already pass if the beat is a discovery.
- What the builder is (SPEC 4 T3, verbatim): "the reader sets a weight $w_i$ on each city and a weight $w_{ij}$ on each joined pair, which defines $Q_w(p)=\sum_i w_i\,p_i^2-\sum_{i<j} w_{ij}\,p_ip_j$. City weights are positive; road weights are zero or positive. "Test" asks the engine (`formIsInvariant`) whether every mutation preserves $Q_w$ and, if not, shows the population and the mutation that the engine returns as a witness."
- What the reader discovers: on $A_2$ the test passes exactly when the two city weights and the road weight are equal (SPEC 3 M2 row 2, the figure table's statement; no check in `reference/verify.py` covers it, so do not mark it [V]: its provenance is the uniqueness result cited at the end of this bullet, [S], and the tool's own acceptance test on $A_2$ is a test of the tool, not a provenance label), which is the form of beat 1 up to scale. In general "On a balanced connected board exactly one choice up to scale passes: $w_i=2d_i$ and $w_{ij}=2d_iA_{ji}$" (SPEC 4 T3; 2.5). On a simple graph $d_i=1$ (2.5 "Simple graphs"), so the recipe is: every city weight 2, every road weight 2, up to a common factor. Provenance for "forced": on a connected board the invariant form is unique up to a constant ([W20] Theorems 8.4 and 8.7 [S]).
- Keep to simple graphs. Balanced boards with unequal $d_i$ and the ratio $2:1$ on $B_2$ are M6's material (P4: "multiple roads arrive in M6"). Do not define "balanced" here beyond, at most, one sentence saying that on a simple graph every city gets the same weight; the word and its condition on cycles belong to M6. Do not mention one-way roads or the unbalanced triangles (M3).

### 5.4 Beat 3: the matrix $2I-A$, a mutation is a reflection

- Idea (beat 3, verbatim): "The matrix of the form is $2I-A$: the Cartan matrix of a simple graph. A mutation is a reflection for this form."
- Figure (table row 3, verbatim): "static: the matrix $2I-A$ for $A_3$ beside the board". The entries are golden values (`types.A3.cartan`, and `types.A3.adjacency` if you show $A$). SPEC 7.3 says of `<Tex src="... {{path}} ..."/>`: "It is the only way a golden value enters a formula", and CONTRACTS 7 says "in formulas through `<Tex src="... {{path}} ..."/>`". So the matrix enters through `{{path}}` inside `<Tex>`, never through TeX source assembled from `resolveGoldenPath` in an `export const` of the MDX. Within the grammar (plan): typeset the three rows with `{{types.A3.cartan[0]}}`, `{{types.A3.cartan[1]}}`, `{{types.A3.cartan[2]}}` stacked in one `<Tex display>` (for example inside `\left(\begin{array}{c} ... \end{array}\right)`), which prints each row as a list such as $(2, -1, 0)$; confirm the exact output at build time. If you judge that a true `pmatrix` with one entry per cell is needed, do not build it outside `<Tex>`: send `P0-A4` a request (under `requests` in your report) for a double index or a matrix output form in the shared resolver, and list any interim workaround under `deviations` with the reason (COMMON 4.4: a change to a `CONTRACTS.md` interface is a deviation that `MERGE` decides). `MatrixView.svelte` is phase 5 (`P5-A2`) and not available. The static figure needs a text alternative (A-10): a caption plus, for example, a visually hidden table or an `aria-label` that reads the rows; KaTeX's MathML (A-12) may serve if the route of `P0-A3` keeps it.
- What the prose says: the form of beat 1 is $(p,q)=p^{\mathsf T}Bq$ with $B=DC$ (2.5); on a simple graph $D=I$ and $A$ is symmetric, so $B=C=2I-A^{\mathsf T}=2I-A$ (2.1, 2.5): the Cartan matrix. Diagonal entries 2 (one Martian has value 2), $-1$ for a joined pair, 0 otherwise. Convention: $C_{ij}=\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle$ (Kac, SageMath; Bourbaki prints the transpose); on a simple graph the matrix is symmetric, so the point is moot here, and the convention is stated in the Lie theory paragraph only if you write the reflection with $C$ there.
- The reflection (2.5, verbatim formula): "A mutation is the reflection $S_ip=p-\frac{2(p,e_i)}{(e_i,e_i)}e_i$ for this form." On a simple graph $(e_i,e_i)=2$, so $S_ip=p-(p,e_i)\,e_i$: the mutation subtracts from city $i$ the pairing of $p$ with one Martian in city $i$, and that pairing is $2p_i$ minus the populations of the neighbours, which is the rule of M1 seen again (2.2). Say what a reflection is for a reader of first-year linear algebra (D2): it fixes the "mirror" of populations whose pairing with $e_i$ is 0 and sends $e_i$ to $-e_i$. Do not call $(p,e_i)$ "the number on node $i$" (P4; that is M4's bridge).
