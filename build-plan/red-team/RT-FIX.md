# RT-FIX: Fixing one cluster of confirmed red-team findings

Applies from phase 0, in every red-team round that confirms a finding (README 5.3, step 4). Size: README and the rosters set none (plan: expect S to M per cluster); the harness runs up to four fixers at once, one per cluster. Runs in: a fresh worktree on branch `fix/P{k}-<cluster>` (`CONTRACTS.md` 10). The harness creates the worktree and the branch; the name that `git branch --show-current` prints is the one you report, exactly as printed.

Before this file you have read `build-plan/COMMON.md`, `build-plan/CONTRACTS.md` and `build-plan/RED-TEAM-COMMON.md`. Everything there applies. Section 7 of `RED-TEAM-COMMON.md` is your brief in five lines; this file is the same brief with the details. For every rule of `COMMON.md` sections 2 to 5 you are a build agent: the conventions, the toolchain rules, the worktree discipline, the forbidden list and the report format all bind you. For the question of what a finding is and what counts as evidence, `RED-TEAM-COMMON.md` sections 2 to 4 bind you too, because your verdicts on findings are read by the same standard. Where this file says (plan), the rule is the build plan's addition and not the specification's.

## 1. Objective

When you are done, every confirmed finding of your cluster has one of three verdicts, each with evidence. `fixed`: the cause is removed from the source for every input, and the reproducer is a permanent test under a plain name that was red before your change and is green after it (the single exception, a missing `DECISIONS.md` entry supplied in your fragment, is defined in 5.5). `needs-decision`: the only honest fix would change something no agent may change (a golden value, a convention of `SPEC.md` section 2, a cap, a comparison rule of 5.7, an interface of `CONTRACTS.md`, a recorded decision, or a file reserved to `MERGE`, `GATE` or the owner), and you say exactly what would have to change and what the options are. `could-not-reproduce`: the behaviour is absent on your branch before any change of yours, and you give the command and output that show it, which sends the finding back to verification. Your branch is green under the engine suite, the area's tests and the type-checks at every commit; every commit names the findings it addresses; `MERGE` can take the branch and know from your report alone what changed and why. The protocol of README 5.3 depends on this step: a confirmed correctness bug becomes a permanent test here or nowhere.

## 3. Inputs you depend on

- A fresh worktree on your branch, based on `main` as it stood when the round's fix step began (the gate commit in round 1, the merge commit of the previous round afterwards). `git rev-parse HEAD` before your first commit is your base; record it.
- The findings of your cluster as JSON, as described in section 2 item 1. The cluster name is the first three path segments of `location` (the harness computes it; `packages/engine/src`, `site/src/components`, `site/tests/e2e` are typical). The `severity` field is the lens's original value: the harness does not apply a verifier's adjustment to it (plan, `workflows/redteam-phase.js`). Read `verification.reproduce.severityAdjustment` and `verification.refute.severityAdjustment` with their `reason`; where either is present, the adjusted severity (the higher of the two when both are present and differ, plan) is the one you use to order your work (section 8 step 3) and the one you state in the acceptance line's evidence, beside the original.
- The lens branches `redteam/P{k}-RT-{nn}`, present as local branches in the same repository, since worktrees share refs. `git show <branch>:<path>` reads a file; `git checkout <branch> -- <path>` brings it into your worktree, staged at its original path.
- `node_modules` at the root (run `npm ci --prefer-offline` once if absent; never `npm install`); Node 22.12 or later; `python3` for running the oracle while you investigate, never inside a test.
- `reference/` unchanged: the three hashes of `CONTRACTS.md` 9.

Stop and report `blocked` with the detail if: the branch is `main`, a `redteam/` branch or an `agent/` branch; a reference hash differs; a `reproducer` path does not exist on the named lens branch and the `reproduction` field gives no commands you can turn into a test; `node_modules` cannot be installed. Do not re-derive a finding from the specification on your own; that is the lens's job, and a finding that cannot be fetched goes back to the orchestrator.

## 5. Requirements

### 5.1 Pre-flight

Run, and record each in `commandsRun` with its summary line:

1. `node --version`: 22.12 or later, else stop and report `blocked` (`COMMON.md` 3).
2. `git rev-parse --show-toplevel` and `git branch --show-current`: the worktree and branch of your report. `git rev-parse HEAD`: your base.
3. `shasum -a 256 reference/golden_data.json reference/lie_games.py reference/verify.py` against `CONTRACTS.md` 9: a difference is `blocked`.
4. `npm ci --prefer-offline` at the root if `node_modules` is absent.
5. `npm test -w packages/engine` before you change anything. Its summary line is the baseline: the pass count after your work must not fall below it, and every golden test passes before and after.

### 5.2 Reading a finding

For each finding, before touching code:

1. Read the `specRef` passage in `SPEC.md` itself. Read the oracle function when the finding is about an engine function. Decide what the specification and the oracle require for every input, not only for the reproducer's input. Write that requirement down in one sentence; it is the sentence your commit body will carry.
2. Read `verification.reproduce.evidence`: the command the verifier ran, the output it saw, the commit it ran on. Your first run of the adopted test must show the same `observed` value; if it does not, say so in the acceptance line and work section 5.6.
3. Treat `proposedFix` as a hypothesis. The lens saw a symptom and guessed; you own the cause. When the proposal is wrong, say so in `handoff` in one sentence, because the lens reruns after your fix and should know.
4. Check the finding once more against the "not a finding" list of `RED-TEAM-COMMON.md` 2, with the citations open: a [D] choice recorded in `DECISIONS.md` or a `decisions/` fragment; an item of a later phase (the phase columns of `SPEC.md` 3, 4, 5.3, 5.4, 5.7 and 9.1); a sentence of `SPEC.md` that the oracle contradicts (0.2 item 2). Two verifiers passed it, so you will usually confirm it. When you do not, the verdict is `needs-decision` with your citation (section 5.5); never a quiet fix in the other direction, never an edit to the reproducer, never a silent drop.
5. Group the findings of the cluster by cause. Two findings with one cause get one fix and one commit naming both ids, and two adopted tests (or one test file with two cases, each above its own `// regression:` comment).

### 5.3 Adopting a reproducer

**Where.** The owning area's permanent test directory, by the area of the fix:

| Area of the fix | Directory | Name |
|---|---|---|
| `packages/engine/src/*`, a non-golden behaviour | `packages/engine/test/unit/` (the directory `P0-A2` opened for the non-golden engine tests `labels`, `overflow`, `moves`) | `<module>-<what>.test.ts`, for example `search-cap-at-layer-end.test.ts` |
| a golden key without a test, or with a weak one | the one golden file that owns the key (`CONTRACTS.md` 3: a key is tested in exactly one file; `grep -rl "<key>" packages/engine/test/golden/` finds it), or a new file in `packages/engine/test/golden/` that calls `covers()` when no file owns it | the owning file, or `p{k}-<keys>.test.ts` (plan: a naming inferred from `P0-A2`'s golden files; `CONTRACTS.md` 3 and `SPEC.md` 5.7 set no file name) |
| a property of 5.7 | `packages/engine/test/properties/p{n}-*.test.ts` of that property: the counterexample added as an example, the recorded seed unchanged | the existing file |
| `site/src/lib/*`, `site/src/components/board/*`, `site/src/components/content/*`, `site/src/components/math/*`, `site/src/components/draw/*`, `site/astro.config.mjs`, `scripts/*`, root configuration, `.github/workflows/*` | `site/tests/unit/` (Vitest) | `<module>-<what>.test.ts`; for a check that reads a repository file (an exact pin, a forbidden string in a workflow, an `engines` field), `repo-<what>.test.ts`. When the fix itself lies in root configuration, root `package.json`, the lockfile or `.github/workflows/*`, the finding is `needs-decision` with a request (5.5) and its test is not committed |
| a tool `T{nn}` | `site/tests/unit/tools/T{nn}.test.ts` or `site/tests/e2e/T{nn}.spec.ts`, in a `describe('regressions')` block at the end, since the contract gives every tool exactly one unit file and one browser file (`CONTRACTS.md` 5.2); every new `data-out` name you read goes into the comment block at the top | the tool's own file |
| a page, a lesson, the run without JavaScript, accessibility, layout, the worker boundary | `site/tests/e2e/` (Playwright); when the check belongs to the sweep of `nojs.spec.ts`, `a11y.spec.ts` or `basepath.spec.ts`, a new `test()` at the end of that file | `<what>.spec.ts`, or the existing sweep file |
| a lesson's text (a typed numeral where `<G>` belongs, a missing wrapper, a forbidden phrase) | `site/tests/unit/` with a test that reads the `.mdx` source and asserts the pattern is present or absent | `content-<what>.test.ts` |

**How.** `git checkout <lens branch> -- <reproducer path>`, then `git mv` to its destination, then edit only what adoption requires:

- the file name, and the import paths that the move breaks;
- the comment `// regression: <finding id>` on the line above `test(` or `it(`, one per finding; keep the finding's `title` as the test name or close to it, so that the suite reads as a list of what went wrong and was fixed;
- fixtures the test needs, moved into `fixtures/` beside it and loaded by a path relative to the test file (`import.meta.url`), never by a path into a `redteam/` directory or into the lens worktree;
- a Playwright reproducer that reads class names, element order or inner structure is rewritten to read only the `data-*` hooks of `SPEC.md` 4.0 and the live region (`CONTRACTS.md` 5.2); the asserted values do not change;
- a reproducer that calls `python3` at test time is rewritten to compare with literal values: run the oracle once yourself, paste the values, and write the oracle call that produced them in a comment above the literal (for example `// oracle: python3 -c "import sys; sys.path.insert(0, 'reference'); import lie_games as L; print(L.orbit_graph_capped(...))"`). The permanent suite never imports the Python (`SPEC.md` 5.7: the engine never reads the Python; the JSON is the contract). Keep the input small enough to read in the test;
- a property-test counterexample (a `fast-check` seed and path) becomes a plain example test with the concrete input and, when the property file exists, the counterexample is also added to that property's examples option (read the installed `fast-check` type definitions for the current name and shape of the option; do not change the recorded seed, do not reduce the run count);
- a script reproducer (`.mjs`, `.py`, or a shell line in `reproduction`) becomes a test that asserts the same expected value on the same input, in the directory of the table above; the script itself stays on the lens branch and is not committed to yours;
- a reproducer that depends on a cap or an arithmetic other than the default keeps that cap and that arithmetic, passed explicitly, so that the test says what it tests.

Nothing else changes. Not the input, not the board, not the cap passed, not the expected value, not the assertion, not the tolerance, not the comparison helper. Section 9 item 1 says why.

**Red first.** Run the adopted test before the fix. It must fail, and the failure message must show the finding's `observed` value. Run it alone, by file, from the repository root and never after a `cd` (`COMMON.md` 4.6): `npm test -w packages/engine -- <file>` for the engine (the form of `RED-TEAM-COMMON.md` 5; in the Vitest command line `-w` means `--watch`, so never write `npx vitest run <file> -w packages/engine`); `npx vitest run --project site-unit <file>` for site unit tests (the root Vitest configuration's site project, named `site-unit` by `P0-A1` 5.12, plan; read the name `P0-A1` recorded in its fragment); `npm run build` once and then `npx playwright test <file>` for a browser test (`playwright.config.ts` is at the root). Paste the failing summary line into the acceptance line's evidence. If the test passes, go to section 5.6 before doing anything else for that finding.

**Missing or weak tests.** When the finding is that a test is missing, tautological, skipped, marked `.only`, loosened, compares a value with itself, or passes against an empty implementation (lenses `RT-11` and `RT-10`), the test is the fix. Write or strengthen it in the file the table names, in the direction of strength only, and prove that it bites: break the function by hand (return an empty array, drop a term, swap two indices, remove the check), run the test red, restore the function, run it green. Say which mutation you used in the acceptance line. Remove a `.only` or a `skip` and run what it hid; if a hidden test now fails, that failure belongs to the same finding and gets a cause fix like any other.

### 5.4 Fixing the cause

1. **Find the cause, not the symptom.** A wrong value on screen whose cause is in `packages/engine/src/search.ts` is fixed in `search.ts`, even when the finding's `location` is a Svelte component and your cluster is `site/src/components`. List every changed file in `filesChanged` and flag in `handoff` every file outside your cluster's area, so that `MERGE` looks for a conflict with another fixer of the round. Never fix a symptom in your area to avoid leaving it.
2. **The fix is general.** It holds for every board, both arithmetics (`SafeInt` and `Big`), every label scheme (`1..n`, `0..n`, custom), both games, and every input kind of the tables of `SPEC.md` 5.6. No condition in the fix mentions the reproducer's board, input, size or name. If you cannot write the fix without such a condition, you have not found the cause yet.
3. **The oracle is the reference for the engine.** Port the oracle's choice (E-5: lowest position first; the orders of candidates and of search; `orbit_graph_capped` for what a cap reports). After the fix the golden tests still pass; if one fails, either the fix is wrong or the finding is a `needs-decision` (section 5.5). Never adjust a golden test, a comparison rule or the harness to make the suite pass.
4. **Look for the sibling.** The same cause usually lives twice: `mutate` and `fire`; `toLabels` and `toPositions`; `orbit`, `orbitGraph` and `roots`; the `SafeInt` and `Big` instances; the mutation and firing announcement templates; the populations and numbers boards of the linked view; `encodeState` and `decodeState`; the main-thread and worker paths of one call. Fix the sibling when its cause is the same, with its own test, and give it its own acceptance line with `item` `"<finding id> (sibling): <what>"`. When the sibling lies in another area and its fix is a separate decision, log it under `openQuestions` instead.
5. **Keep the fix small.** No refactor the cause does not need, no renaming, no reformatting around the fix, no new abstraction, no comment rewrite. `MERGE` reads diffs; a hundred changed lines for a one-line cause is a defect of your work, and it widens the conflict with another fixer.
6. **Stay inside `COMMON.md` 4.4 and `SPEC.md` 9.3 while fixing.** No new dependency; no `any` without a comment; no `console.log`; Svelte 5 runes only (a bug in a Svelte 5 component is never fixed with `export let`, `$:` or `on:click`); `astro build` and `astro preview`, never `astro dev`; no em-dash anywhere, comments and commit messages included; no floating point in an integer path (E-1); functions stay pure (E-5); the `classify` cache changes no result (E-11); `node_modules` type definitions before any API you are not certain of (`COMMON.md` 3).
7. **Minor findings** in the cluster are "fixed if the fixer is already in the file, else logged" (README 5.3): fix one only when it lies in a file you are already changing for another finding of the cluster, and adopt or write its test like any other. A minor finding in any other file is logged, not fixed, however short its fix. The log is the acceptance line itself: the verdict `needs-decision` with the reason "left for the owner: minor, outside the files changed" (plan: the acceptance schema has no fourth status, and README 5.3 does not say where "logged" is written).
8. **A bug nobody confirmed** is not yours to fix, unless it has the same cause as a finding you are fixing (then the fix covers it and you add a test for it). Write every other one in `openQuestions` with the input and the observed value, so that the next lens round reaches it through the protocol. A fix that no verifier has confirmed a finding for is a change without a check, which the protocol does not allow.

### 5.5 What you never change, and the verdict `needs-decision`

You never change, in any fix:

- a value in `reference/` (the three files are read-only for everyone, `COMMON.md` 2; their hashes are in `CONTRACTS.md` 9);
- a convention of `SPEC.md` section 2 (the Cartan matrix and its transpose, the direction of roads, the two moves, the sign of the legal move, labels and their order, `B2` not `C2`, lowest position first);
- a cap: the table of `SPEC.md` 5.6, every constant of `CAPS` in `packages/engine/src/caps.ts`, the 2000 of E-9, the default `layers` of 40, `relabellingsDefault`, `legalLayersDefault`, the 25 ms and 100 ms thresholds and the size limits of 7.6;
- a comparison rule of `SPEC.md` 5.7 or its implementation in `packages/engine/test/golden/_harness.ts` (`intEq`, `ringsEq`, `ratText`, `matchRecord`, `sortedPositiveRoots`), `GOLDEN_KEYS_BY_PHASE`, the rule that a key is covered by exactly one file;
- an interface of `CONTRACTS.md`: a signature of 2.4, a type of 2.2, the module that owns a function in 2.1, an export of the harness in 3, a function of 4, a prop or hook of 5.1 and 5.2, an exact string of 5.3 (and so of `SPEC.md` 7.5 and 4.0), a preset id of 6, a frontmatter field or component name of 7, a CSS custom property of 8, a branch or commit form of 10. An added optional parameter or optional field is also a change;
- a recorded decision: a [D] choice that a `decisions/` fragment or `DECISIONS.md` records, a default of section 10 that is in use;
- a sentence of `SPEC.md` that is printed on the site (7.5, 4.0, the bridge sentence of 2.4(a)), by rewording;
- `DECISIONS.md`, `CLAUDE.md`, `content/unverified.json`, the licences, `deploy.yml`, `links.yml`, root `package.json`, `package-lock.json`, the dependency lists of `packages/engine/package.json` and `site/package.json`, anything that needs `npm install`;
- a test, in the direction of weakness: no removed assertion, no loosened tolerance, no `skip`, no `.only`, no changed expected value, no comparison helper swapped for a looser one (`COMMON.md` 4.4).

When the only fix that would make the adopted test pass needs one of these, the verdict is `needs-decision`. Stop work on that cause; leave the source as it was for it (revert any partial change before committing); do not commit the adopted test, so that your branch stays green (the reproducer stays on the lens branch and the acceptance line names it there). In the acceptance line's evidence, name the fixed thing exactly: the golden key and both values; the convention sentence; the `CAPS` constant and the two numbers; the comparison rule by number; the `CONTRACTS.md` section and symbol; the fragment that records the choice. Give the two or more options the owner or `MERGE` has and your recommendation in one sentence. When the engine disagrees with the oracle and you believe the oracle is wrong, follow `COMMON.md` 2 exactly: write `reports/discrepancies/RT-FIX-P{k}-<cluster slug>-<n>.py`, importing `lie_games` and demonstrating the point, commit it, name it in the line, and continue with the other findings.

One case that looks like `needs-decision` and is within your reach (plan): a missing `DECISIONS.md` entry for a default another agent used (`SPEC.md` 9.3: "add a line to `DECISIONS.md` with the date and the reason") is supplied by writing the entry in your own fragment, saying whose default it records; `MERGE` folds fragments into `DECISIONS.md`. The verdict is `fixed`, and this is the one `fixed` finding without a regression test, because the defect is a missing record and not a behaviour: the `test` field is `decisions/RT-FIX-P{k}-<cluster slug>.md > <entry heading>`, the red run is a `grep` over `DECISIONS.md` and `decisions/` on your base that prints nothing for the default, and the green run is the same `grep` on your branch printing the new entry (section 6, line 2 names this exception).

A dependency, pin or `engines` finding whose fix is an edit to root `package.json` or the lockfile is `needs-decision` with the exact edit written out under `requests`, for `MERGE` to make, since `MERGE` is on the list of those who may edit them (README 6).

The files `.gitignore`, `playwright.config.ts`, the root Vitest configuration and `ci.yml` are owned by `P0-A6` and then by `MERGE` and `GATE` only (README 6), and "an agent whose task needs a change in another agent's area writes the request in its report and its decisions fragment, and the owning agent or `MERGE` makes the change" (README 6). You do not edit them. A finding whose fix lies in one of them (a removed `astro dev`, a missing include pattern, an ignored path) is `needs-decision`: write the exact edit (file, line, old text, new text) under `requests` and in your fragment, name the request in the acceptance line, and do not commit the adopted test, which would leave your branch red until `MERGE` makes the edit (plan: the verdict that puts the edit in `MERGE`'s hands). A fix in `CLAUDE.md`, the licences, `deploy.yml` or `links.yml` is `needs-decision` as well: those change only by `GATE` on the owner's instruction.

The source files of `packages/engine/src/`, `site/src/` and `scripts/` in which a cause lies are yours to change even when README 6 gives the area to another agent, because README 5.3 step 4 makes the fixer the one who "fixes the cause" (plan: the reading of README 6 for `RT-FIX`; README 6 does not list `RT-FIX`). This includes `packages/engine/src/index.ts`, owned by `P0-A2`, only to export a name that `CONTRACTS.md` 2.4 already lists and the index omits; you list that edit under `deviations`. Exporting a name that `CONTRACTS.md` does not list is an interface change and so `needs-decision`.

### 5.6 The verdict `could-not-reproduce`

When the adopted test passes on your base before any change of yours: first check that it asserts what the finding says (the same board, input, cap and arithmetic; the `observed` value of the verifier's evidence would be the failure message) and that you ran it the way `reproduction` says (a build before a browser test; the right workspace; the right environment variable). Then run `git log --oneline <gate commit>..HEAD -- <the file in location>` for a change since the gate commit, which happens when another round's fix or another cluster's merge reached the same cause. If the test still passes, the verdict is `could-not-reproduce`, with: the command, its summary line, your base commit, the verifier's commit from `verification.reproduce.evidence`, and what you checked. Commit the adopted test only if you have shown that it bites: break the function the finding names by hand, run the test red, restore it, run it green, and say so in the line. A test that has never been seen red is not evidence and does not go on your branch. The finding returns to verification; you do not argue it away and you do not mark it fixed.

### 5.7 Running the checks

After every cause fix, and once more from a clean working tree before you report:

| Scope | Command | When |

## 9. Traps

1. **Editing the reproducer.** The reproducer is the finding's evidence, confirmed by two agents from a clean checkout. The moment its input, expected value, cap, board or assertion changes, your green run proves nothing, and `RT-11` reads the diff of your branch in the next round. Adoption changes names, paths, selectors and the fixture location, nothing else (5.3). When you believe the reproducer is wrong, the verdict is `needs-decision` with your citation (5.5) or `could-not-reproduce` with your run (5.6). "The expected value in the test was off by one, so I corrected it" is the sentence that ends a fixer's credibility.

2. **Hiding a bug behind a cap.** A search that reports `completeLayers` wrongly can be "fixed" by stopping one layer earlier; an overflow in `SafeInt` by catching `EngineOverflow` and returning `truncated: true`; a greedy descent that loops by letting `isRootPopulation` reach 20000 steps and answer `null`; a `playFate` that misses a repeat by letting it run to `cap`; a slow enumeration by lowering `maxDepth` or the items passed. Each hides the bug behind the words "stopped after", which the reader then believes. A cap is reached only when the specification's quantity is exhausted; reaching it is reported in a result field and never thrown (E-10), and an error is never reported as a cap. Never change a `CAPS` constant, a default `layers`, the 2000 of E-9 or a threshold of 7.6, and never pass a smaller cap in a test to make it faster (`COMMON.md` 4.5). The golden `layer_growth_examples` at 2000 and 100000 and the oracle's `orbit_graph_capped` are the authority on what a cap reports; run them after every fix in `search.ts`.

3. **Changing behaviour on one-way or non-finite boards.** The two tables of `SPEC.md` 5.6 say what every function does off the finite types, and `CONTRACTS.md` 2.5 repeats the exceptions. A guard added to make a finite-type fix tidy (`requireGcm` in `orbit`, `requireFinite` in `toNumbers`) breaks `one_way_counterexample` and the sandbox. A `null` turned into a throw, or a throw into a `null`, changes the contract of every tool that handles it. A `Number()` applied to a `bigint` to make arithmetic easy silently loses precision past move 39 of `bigint_test`; a `Math.*` call in an integer path breaks E-1. Deciding "is this board finite?" by running an enumeration into its cap instead of calling `classify` breaks the last sentence of 5.6. A fix tested only on the reproducer's named finite board misses the affine board whose labels start at 0 (E-7) and the custom board whose arithmetic is `Big` (E-2). Test every engine fix on four boards at least: the reproducer's; an affine one (`A2~`, or the triangle of `layer_growth_examples`); the one-way board of `one_way_counterexample`; the two-node board of `bigint_test` in `Big`. Before phase 2 remember that `classify` does not exist, so every custom board counts as not of finite type (E-9, E-11), and no fix may import the guards.

4. **Breaking another tool's contract.** A fix in `site/src/lib/a11y.ts` rewrites every announcement; one in `state.ts` or `urlCodec.ts` changes every link and must keep old links readable (6.6: old schema versions stay readable for as long as the site exists); one in `Board.svelte` or `layeredLayout.ts` changes the hit areas and the arrow-key model of every board; one in `ToolFrame.svelte` changes the hooks every Playwright test reads; one in `worker.ts` can move a call onto the main thread and break E-9, or into the worker and break the rule that no default state fetches the worker (7.6); one in a tool's imports can drag the whole engine into its chunk and break the 90 kB limit. A renamed `data-out`, a changed `ToolState` field, an exact string of 7.5 reworded by one character, a changed signature of `CONTRACTS.md` 2.4 (an added optional parameter included), a changed preset id, a changed CSS custom property: each is an interface change, so `needs-decision`, not a fix. When the fix is in a shared library or component, run the unit tests of that library and the browser tests of every tool the findings name, plus `a11y.spec.ts` and `nojs.spec.ts` (5.7).

5. **Fixing the symptom.** A stray `+ 1` on a label is fixed where the conversion is wrong (`toLabels`, `toPositions`, or the one call site that converted twice), never by subtracting one in the component that showed it (E-7: only two functions convert). A wrong number in an announcement is fixed in the template or in the value handed to it, never by a string replacement at the live region. A failing golden comparison is fixed in the function, never in the test. When the finding's `location` is where the symptom appears, expect the cause one or two calls deeper, often in another agent's file.

6. **Special-casing.** `if (board.name === 'B2')`, `if (cap === 2000)`, `if (n <= 2)`, a lookup table that contains the reproducer's input, an examples list in the source: each makes the test pass and leaves the bug. A fix that mentions the reproducer's data is a confession.

7. **The oracle is the reference, both ways.** A finding that cites a sentence of `SPEC.md` against the oracle's value should have been refuted (0.2 item 2); if it reached you, do not move the engine away from the oracle: `needs-decision`, with the oracle function and the golden key. A finding that cites the oracle against the engine is fixed to the oracle even where a sentence of `SPEC.md` reads otherwise, and the sentence goes into `openQuestions` for the owner. Never edit `verify.py` or `lie_games.py` to settle either case; the discrepancy script of `COMMON.md` 2 is the channel.

8. **Making a golden test pass.** The harness is the comparison rules of 5.7; a regression test never re-implements a rule (`CONTRACTS.md` 3), never uses `toBeCloseTo` where `intEq` belongs, never sorts a list whose order has meaning, never compares a record by index, never calls `covers()` for a key another file covers. If your fix makes a golden test fail, the golden test is right and the fix is wrong, or the finding is a `needs-decision`.

9. **The two eras of `classify`.** In a phase 0 or 1 cluster, `classify`, `requireFinite` and the other guards do not exist; a fix must not import them, and a phase 1 tool calls the functions of the finite theory only on named finite boards (E-11). From phase 2, a fix that calls `classify` inside a hot path (a move, a render) instead of once at board load defeats the cache of E-11 and the 10 ms rule of E-9; the verdict is cached per board object in a `WeakMap`, so hand the same board object on, do not rebuild it.

10. **`Big` and `SafeInt`.** The two instances share one code path (E-2). A fix made in one and not the other is half a fix: run the adopted test with `fire` in `Big` on the `bigint_test` board and in `SafeInt` on `A2`. Mixing `number` and `bigint` throws `TypeError: Cannot mix BigInt and other types`; `JSON.stringify` throws on `bigint`; `-0` must normalise to `0`. Mind the overflow move: a `SafeInt` that checks every operation throws during move 38 of `bigint_test`, because the product is formed before the safe result (the trap list of `P0-A2` explains it); a finding that expects 39 there was misread and is a `needs-decision`, not a weakened `SafeInt`.

11. **Timing and size findings.** The 25 ms benchmark, the 100 ms drawing test, a size limit of 7.6 and a lab timing are fixed by removing work (an allocation in a loop, a hash computed twice, an import that pulls the whole engine into a tool's chunk, KaTeX reaching the client), never by raising the threshold, shrinking the measured case, or rerunning the measurement until it passes once. Report the median you measured and how.

12. **Svelte 5 and Astro 7.** A component bug is not fixed with Svelte 4 idioms (`export let`, `$:`, `on:click`, `writable` for local state, slots where snippets are expected); read the installed type definitions before touching a rune you are unsure of (`COMMON.md` 3). Moving a tool from `client:visible` to `client:load` to make hydration deterministic breaks 7.6. Adding a remark or rehype plugin to fix a content rendering finding changes the mathematics route recorded by `P0-A3` and is `needs-decision`. Fixing a build-time check by switching to `astro dev` is forbidden outright.

13. **Exact strings.** The templates of 7.5 and the stated-limit phrases of 4.0 (`CONTRACTS.md` 5.3) are compared character for character by `RT-05` and `RT-10`. "minus 1", not "-1"; "Node 3" in the numbers game and "City 2" in the mutation game; "stopped after 2000 positions; layers 0 to 10 are complete", with the semicolon; "Game over after 6 moves." with the full stop. A fix that makes a sentence read better is a new finding.

14. **Positions and labels in tests.** The golden file and every URL hold labels; the engine holds positions. An adopted test that subtracts 1 by hand to turn a label into a position is wrong on affine boards, where label 0 is position 0; use `toPositions` and `toLabels` (`labels()` and `wordLabels()` of the harness in engine tests). A fix that adds a `+ 1` anywhere in `src/` is almost always wrong (E-7).

15. **Fixtures and paths.** A test that reads `../../redteam/...`, an absolute path into the lens worktree, or a path relative to `process.cwd()` breaks in `MERGE`'s checkout and in CI. Load fixtures by `import.meta.url` relative to the test file, as the harness loads the golden file.

16. **Discovery.** A test file in a directory that no include pattern covers passes by never running. Run the adopted file by name and read the test count in the summary; then run the area's full command and confirm the count rose by the number of tests you added. If the pattern must change, the root Vitest configuration is not yours to edit (README 6): the finding is `needs-decision` with the exact include pattern under `requests` for `MERGE` (5.5).

17. **Two clusters, one cause.** The harness clusters by the first three path segments of `location`, so one cause can appear in two clusters with two fixers, and you cannot see the other cluster. Fix the cause where it is, flag the file in `handoff`, keep the commit small so that `MERGE`'s conflict is small, and never try to pick up the other fixer's work by merging.

18. **Leaving the branch red.** A committed adopted test for a `needs-decision` finding fails on your branch and would fail `main` after the merge; `MERGE` would then have to drop your whole branch. The reproducer stays on the lens branch; your branch is green at every commit. The same holds for a `could-not-reproduce` test that you have not seen red under a deliberate break.

19. **Fixing what nobody confirmed.** A bug you notice beside the one you are fixing is tempting and quick. Unless it has the same cause, it goes into `openQuestions` (5.4 item 8). The protocol's value is that every change on `main` traces to a verified finding and a test; a side fix without either weakens it, and the lens that reruns on your area would not know to check your side fix.

20. **Marking your own finding fixed anywhere but in your report.** You do not edit `reports/`, a lens branch, a findings file or `DECISIONS.md`, and you do not write "fixed" in a commit message of another finding. The acceptance line is the only place; the lens rerun and `GATE` decide whether the fix held.

21. **Em-dashes.** Forbidden everywhere you write: source comments, test names, commit messages, the fragment, the discrepancy script, the report (`COMMON.md` 4.4; `SPEC.md` 8.4). Check the diff before you commit.
