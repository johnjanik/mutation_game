# RT-09: math-claims

Applies from phase 1. Runs in: a worktree on branch `redteam/P{k}-RT-09`.

Before this file you have read `build-plan/RED-TEAM-COMMON.md` and `build-plan/CONTRACTS.md`. Everything there applies.

## 1. Mission

This lens checks that what the site says about mathematics is true. Every mathematical sentence of a lesson, a page, a tool caption, a verdict, a script caption, a prediction, a challenge and a fallback is checked against section 2 of `SPEC.md` and against the oracle and the golden data; every figure of a lesson is checked against its row in the figure tables of section 3 (board, view, mode, start, the result stated); every "For readers who know Lie theory" paragraph is checked for the right standard names; and every result quoted from an author with another convention is checked for a correct translation (8.4, 2.9). A build can pass every gate while saying false things: the golden tests check the engine, not the prose; the lessons of the core path use $A_2$, $A_3$, $D_4$ and $E_8$, on which $C$ is symmetric, roots and coroots coincide, marks equal comarks, every root has form value 2 and every raising move raises by 1, so a formula that is transposed, a statement about roots that should be about coroots, or a fact of simply laced boards stated for all boards prints correct numbers on every figure and is still false. If this lens misses, a reader who goes on to $B_3$ or to the Sandbox finds the site contradicting itself, and the secondary reader, who knows the theory, stops trusting it.

## 4. Attack list

Severity follows `RED-TEAM-COMMON.md` 4: "a mathematical statement presented as fact that is wrong" is a **blocker**, whether it is in prose, a caption, a verdict or a fallback; a figure whose configuration differs from section 3 while the page still states section 3's result is a blocker (the page then says something its own figure does not show); a figure that differs from section 3 and states its own, true result is not a finding (the tables "may be refined [D]"); a missing Lie theory name or an imprecise but not false wording is **minor**. Each attack lists the phases in which it applies. "Probe boards" means the set of Methods 5.4: $B_2$, $G_2$, $B_3$, $C_3$, the one-way road `[[2,0],[-1,2]]`, the triangle with a tail, `B3~`, `G2~`, `A2^(2)`.

1. **A fact of simply laced or finite boards stated for all boards (phases 1 to 7).** What: every general sentence (Methods 5.2 marks sentences with "every", "always", "never", "any", "all", "exactly", "only", "on a board", "each move") in lessons, `Seen` boxes, captions, goal notes and verdicts. How: for each, write down its hypothesis as printed, then test it with the oracle on the probe boards (Methods 5.3). The known scopes, each with its counterexample: "every raising move raises a city by exactly 1", "every play of Kostant's game has $h-2$ moves" and "ends at the highest root" hold on simply laced finite boards only (F13): on $B_2$ the plays have 1 move, not $h-2=2$, and on $B_3$ they have 3 moves from cities 1 and 2 and 2 moves from city 3 (`kostant.B3.moves_from_node` is `[3, 3, 2]`), ending at $(1,1,1)$ from the short city; off the finite types a rise can be any positive amount (the triangle, $(1,1,0)\to(1,1,2)$, 2.16); "every root population has form value 2" holds on simply laced boards only (on $B_2$, $(1,0)$ and $(1,2)$ have value 4, 2.5); "the positive roots are exactly the non-negative integer populations of form value 2" holds on simply laced boards of finite type only (on the triangle with a tail, $(1,1,1,0,1)$ has value 2 and is not a root population, M2 beat 5); "Martians and anti-Martians never mix" holds on bidirected boards only (2.11; the one-way road reaches $(-1,1)$ by mutating 2 then 1, `one_way_counterexample`); "the generic game has $N$ moves" holds from starts with all numbers positive (F4; from a fundamental weight it has $N-N_J$, F10); "every position is a population" holds only where $\det C=1$ ($E_8$, $F_4$, $G_2$, 2.4(c)); "twice each city equals the sum of its neighbours" for the frozen population holds on simply laced boards (2.12 says "every city is exactly balanced by its neighbours"); "join a new city to the cities that end with a positive number" is the whole recipe on simply laced boards only (2.12); "$e_x+\delta$ is a root population for every city" holds on untwisted boards only (`twisted_affine[name=A2^(2)].least_k_with_e_x_plus_k_delta_a_root` is `[1, 2]`); "$-w_0$ reverses the nodes" holds on $A_n$ only (2.8); "every ring holds $h$ roots" holds only for the types listed in 2.14. ### 5.3 The oracle checker

`<wt>/redteam/RT-09/check.py`, run as `python3 -I <wt>/redteam/RT-09/check.py <wt>/reference <wt>/redteam/RT-09/checks.json`. Each check names an oracle call in positions and an expected value written as on the page (labels where the page shows labels); the script converts, runs, and prints every mismatch with the claim id.

```python
#!/usr/bin/env python3
"""check.py: runs oracle checks for RT-09. checks.json is a list of
{"id": "M1-beat3-E8", "fn": "kostant_play", "board": "E8", "args": [0], "pick": "end_moves",
 "expect": [[2,3,4,6,5,4,3,2], 28], "claim": "on E8 the end is (2,3,4,6,5,4,3,2) after 28 moves"}
"board" is a name for lie_games.board_from_name, a Cartan matrix (list of rows), or null for the functions
that take a letter and a rank instead of a matrix (satake_catalogue, match_catalogue); args are positions.
"tuple_args" lists the indices of args to pass as tuples (tau). "keys" is used by the picks "keys" and "each".
Rational values (form values, symmetrizer entries, root coordinates of a weight) are written in expect
as integers when integral (2, not "2") and as strings "p/q" otherwise ("3/2"); norm reads both sides alike."""
import sys, json, os, re
from fractions import Fraction
sys.path.insert(0, os.path.abspath(sys.argv[1]))
import lie_games as lg  # noqa: E402

NO_MATRIX = ('satake_catalogue', 'match_catalogue')

def board(b):
    return [list(r) for r in b] if isinstance(b, list) else [list(r) for r in lg.board_from_name(b)[0]]

def norm(x):
    # one form for both sides: an integral value (int or Fraction) becomes an int,
    # any other rational becomes the string "p/q"; a string "p/q" or "-p/q" in expect is read the same way
    if isinstance(x, bool):
        return x
    if isinstance(x, str) and re.fullmatch(r'-?\d+/\d+', x):
        x = Fraction(x)
    if isinstance(x, (int, Fraction)):
        x = Fraction(x)
        return x.numerator if x.denominator == 1 else f"{x.numerator}/{x.denominator}"
    if isinstance(x, (list, tuple)):
        return [norm(v) for v in x]
    if isinstance(x, dict):
        return {k: norm(v) for k, v in x.items()}
    return x

PICK = {
    'end_moves': lambda r, c: [list(r[0]), len(r[1])],            # kostant_play, raising_play
    'end_word_fired': lambda r, c: [list(r[0]), [i + 1 for i in r[1]], list(r[2])],  # play; word as labels
    'status_moves': lambda r, c: [r['status'], r['moves']],       # play_fate
    'conditions': lambda r, c: [r['condition_1'], r['condition_2'], r['condition_3'], r['admissible']],  # admissibility
    'keys': lambda r, c: [r[k] for k in c['keys']],               # one dict result (satake): the named fields only
    'each': lambda r, c: [[e[k] for k in c['keys']] for e in r],  # a list of dicts (satake_catalogue, match_catalogue)
    'value': lambda r, c: r,
}

bad = 0
for c in json.load(open(sys.argv[2])):
    args = [tuple(a) if i in c.get('tuple_args', []) else a for i, a in enumerate(c['args'])]
    fn = getattr(lg, c['fn'])
    if c['fn'] in NO_MATRIX:
        got = fn(*args)                                   # letter, rank, then X and tau for match_catalogue
    elif c['fn'] in ('bil',):
        got = fn(lg.gram(board(c['board'])), *args)
    else:
        got = fn(board(c['board']), *args)
    got = norm(PICK[c.get('pick', 'value')](got, c))
    if json.dumps(got) != json.dumps(norm(c['expect'])):
        bad += 1
        print(f"MISMATCH {c['id']}: page says {c['expect']}, oracle gives {got}  ({c['claim']})")
print(f"{bad} mismatches")
```

`board_from_name` returns finite boards with labels 1 to n and affine boards with node 0 first, as SPEC E-7 says; `cartan(letter, n)` gives the finite matrix directly. Functions that need more than the matrix take positions for `X` and `tau`: `theta(C, X, tau, p)`, `admissibility(C, X, tau)` and `satake(C, X, tau)` take the board as their first argument like the others; `satake_catalogue(L, n)` and `match_catalogue(L, n, X, tau)` take a letter and a rank, so their checks set `"board": null` and put the letter and rank first in `args` (for example `{"fn": "match_catalogue", "board": null, "args": ["A", 3, [1], [2,1,0]], "tuple_args": [3], "pick": "each", "keys": ["algebra"], "expect": [["su(1,3)"]]}`). Give `tau` as a list of positions and name its index in `tuple_args`. Dict results are never compared whole, because they carry reason strings in prose: `admissibility` is compared through the pick `conditions`, which gives `[condition_1, condition_2, condition_3, admissible]` as booleans (the lights of T13 in order, then the verdict); `satake` through `keys` with the fields the page prints (for example `"keys": ["real_rank", "dim_k", "character"]`, expect `[1, 9, -3]` for $\mathfrak{su}(1,3)$); the catalogue functions, which return lists of records, through `each` with the fields to compare (`algebra`, `X`, `tau`, `et`, `em`, `dk` as `satake_catalogue` documents them). The `reasons` sentences are read by eye when a page quotes one. The values of the attacks were computed this way on this machine, and this script was run on samples of each kind: `bil` on $A_3$ with the positions `[1,1,0]` and `[1,1,0]` and expect `2` gives no mismatch, and on $B_2$ with `[1,0]` twice and expect `4` gives none (integral `Fraction` results compare equal to integer expects, and a non-integral value is written as a string such as `"3/2"`); `kostant_play(cartan('B',3), 2)` ends at `(1, 1, 1)` after 2 moves; `admissibility` on $E_6$ with `X` the positions `[2,3,4,5]` and the identity gives `[true, false, true, false]` under `conditions` (condition 2 fails, attack 10), and with the positions `[1,2,3,4]` (the labels 2, 3, 4, 5) gives all four true; `satake_catalogue('D', 4)` under `each` with `algebra` gives the six records `so(1,7)`, `so(2,6)`, `so(3,5)`, `so(4,4)`, `so*(8)`, `so(8)` (M9 beat 7).

## 6. Not a finding for this lens

- A figure configuration that differs from the table of section 3 while the page states a result that is true of the figure shown: section 3 says "The configurations are a starting point for the lesson author and may be refined [D]". Record it in coverage.
- A statement of SPEC that the oracle contradicts, or a golden value that disagrees with the text of section 2: the oracle wins (SPEC 0.2 item 2); report it as a **note** for the owner, not against the build.
- A statement that is true but stated without its provenance, a [U] statement printed outside `<Unverified>`, a golden number typed instead of `<G>`, a `<G>` path to the wrong field that prints the right digits, the bridge sentence missing or reworded, stop messages that differ in wording, "not of finite type" inferred from a cap as a wording rule, the careful wording of a failed condition 3, credit and attribution, the Appendix F statements as a list: `RT-08`. Report here only when the statement is false.
- Engine values on inputs the golden data does not hold, compared with the oracle as functions: `RT-01`. Report here when a page or tool **states** such a value.
- Transposition or label errors in the engine source: `RT-02`. Report here the printed formula and the figure.
- Caps, truncation flags and error classes as engine behaviour: `RT-03`. The live-region templates and accessible-name wording: `RT-05`. Whether an acceptance criterion is automated: `RT-10`.
- "The numbers fired from $\rho$, sorted, are the heights of the positive roots": true as a statement about the sorted list (A.1, last paragraph); a finding only if it is stated value by value or for the linear forms (attack 3).
- "$P/Q$ is the fundamental group of the adjoint group" and the other Lie theory lines that SPEC 3 itself gives for a module: they are the standard; a lesson that repeats them is not a finding.
- $B_2$ and $C_2$ shown under the name the reader chose in a picker (D19 default: "the name of the board the reader chose in pickers, and "$B_2=C_2$" wherever a board is recognised from its shape").
- A module, tool, page or function of a later phase (section 3 of this file); a T2 or T9 verdict before phase 2; M0 and M1 without the sandbox link in phase 1.
- Floating-point display values (Coxeter plane coordinates, "make uniform", radii) that differ from the oracle beyond the sixth decimal while the counts and the rings agree: SPEC 5.5 "tests therefore check invariants ..., never individual coordinates".
- A [D] choice recorded in `DECISIONS.md` (preset captions' wording, the order of panels, which example a lesson adds), unless the choice states something false.
