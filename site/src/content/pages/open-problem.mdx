## The question

The mutation game is N. J. Wildberger's, and it comes with a question that is still open. The paper <Cite k="W20"/> states it in its section {'2'} (page {'57'}, without a number) for any multigraph: every root population consists entirely of Martians or entirely of anti-Martians. In other words, a population that can be reached from a single Martian never holds <Term a="martians">Martians</Term> in one city and <Term a="anti-martians">anti-Martians</Term> in another. The unpublished manuscript <Cite k="W-MS"/> asks the same thing for simple graphs, on page {'2'}, under the name "The Mutation Problem". The earlier paper <Cite k="W03a"/> records the statement for simple graphs as a proposition and remarks that its author knows no direct combinatorial proof. In <Cite k="W20"/> Wildberger adds that the statement might also follow from an analysis of Coxeter groups, which he credits to discussions with Bob Howlett, and he asks for a proof in the language of graphs and combinatorics.

## A road in one direction only

Read word for word, with roads allowed to run one way only, the statement is false. This counts against the statement as <Cite k="W20"/> writes it, because that paper defines a directed multigraph as any multiset of directed edges (section {'4'}). A single road from <Term a="city">city</Term> 1 to city 2, with no road back, is therefore a multigraph in its sense.

Take that board and start with one Martian in city 1, the <Term a="population">population</Term> <G k="one_way_counterexample.start"/>. Mutate the cities in the order <G k="one_way_counterexample.moves" as="word"/>. The first mutation gives (1, 1); the second gives <G k="one_way_counterexample.result"/>, with an anti-Martian in city 1 and a Martian in city 2. On this <Term a="board">board</Term> the root populations do not even form a finite set (verified by computation).

The paper itself does not rely on such boards. Sections {'5'}, {'6'} and {'9'} of <Cite k="W20"/>, and the theorems of its section {'8'}, assume a connected multigraph whose <Term a="roads">roads</Term> run both ways. Section {'8'} begins with a general directed multigraph and observes that a road in one direction forces one of the weights of its invariant form to be zero (read in the paper itself). So this site reads the conjecture for bidirected boards, where every road has a road back. That reading is the site's interpretation of the statement, not something the paper says in these words.

## Boards with roads both ways

On a bidirected board the statement is true. A <Term a="root-population">root population</Term> is a real root of the Kac-Moody algebra of the board's Cartan matrix, and every root of such an algebra is a combination of the simple roots whose coefficients are all at least zero or all at most zero. This holds for every generalized <Term a="cartan-matrix">Cartan matrix</Term>, whether or not it has an <Term a="invariant">invariant</Term> form. <Unverified item="E4" fallback="The statement is standard in the theory of Kac-Moody algebras.">It is a standard result of the theory, found in Kac's book on infinite-dimensional Lie algebras <Cite k="Kac90"/>.</Unverified>

The site has also checked the statement by computation: on every board of <Term a="finite">finite</Term> type of rank at most eight; on the first 2500 or so root populations of every untwisted affine board of rank at most eight; on the same number for each of <G k="twisted_affine" as="count"/> twisted <Term a="affine">affine</Term> boards; and on the boards of the non-crystallographic groups H2, H3 and H4. These checks were run by the site's own scripts: they are verified by computation and are not taken from a source.

## What remains open

What remains open, in Wildberger's sense, is a proof that uses only the game: a combinatorial reason, stated in terms of cities, roads and mutations, why Martians and anti-Martians never mix in a population that started from one Martian. The algebra above does not give one.

## A test for root populations

The algebraic reading gives an exact test of whether a population is a root population, on any <Term a="bidirected-board">bidirected board</Term>. Take a population with no negative city that is not a single Martian. If it is a root population, some city has a positive number, and mutating there lowers the population and leaves a root population with no negative city. So lower it greedily: the population is a root population exactly when this reaches a single Martian without any city going negative and without getting stuck. The site checked the test against a breadth-first search of the <Term a="mutation-game">mutation game</Term> (verified by computation). <Unverified item="E4" fallback="The two facts behind the test were checked by computation on the boards of this site.">The two facts it rests on, that real roots have one sign and that a positive real root other than a simple one is lowered by some simple reflection, are standard <Cite k="Kac90"/>.</Unverified>
