## The games and the story

The mutation game is N. J. Wildberger's (<Cite k="W20"/>, <Cite k="W-MS"/>). So are the words city and population for its board and positions, and the story told with them: Martians and anti-Martians living in the cities of Mars, and Venus for the dual game. This site retells that story with his credit, from <Cite k="W20"/> section 2 and <Cite k="W-MS"/> pp. 1 and 30, and uses his terminology throughout.

In his story every node of a board is a city on Mars, holding a population of Martians, or of anti-Martians when its count is negative. When a city mutates, its Martians become anti-Martians and its anti-Martians become Martians, and it then receives one copy of the population of every other city for each road from that city into it (<Cite k="W20"/>, section 2; <Cite k="W-MS"/>, p. 1; for simple graphs also <Cite k="W03a"/>, section 3). The dual game is told on Venus (<Cite k="W-MS"/>, p. 30). From Wildberger the site takes the game itself, its name, the words city and population, the Martians and anti-Martians, and the dual game. The names Populations and Numbers for the two views of a board are this site's own.

</section>

## The numbers game and Kostant's game

The numbers game is Mozes' (<Cite k="Moz90"/>), with Proctor's earlier use of the same process (<Cite k="Pro84"/>) and Eriksson's development of it (<Cite k="Eri92"/>) acknowledged.

Kostant's game, the mutation game started from one Martian in which a city may mutate only when the mutation raises its population, is attributed to Kostant; the notes <Cite k="Ele16"/> and <Cite k="Che17"/> describe it. {history ? <>The <a href={history}>history page</a> gives the timeline of this work in order of date.</> : null}
