<Beat n={1}>

A population is a list of whole numbers, and the form of module two measures it: on a board of finite type every nonzero population has a positive <Term a="form-value">form value</Term>. That form is not the dot product, so a population is not yet a point to draw. Write $B=DC$ for its matrix, $D$ holding the <Term a="weights">weights</Term> $d_i$, and find $E$ with <Tex src="E^{\mathsf T}E=B"/>. Draw the population $p$ at <Tex src="y=Ep"/>; then <Tex src="y\cdot y'=(p,p')"/>, so the picture keeps the lengths and angles of the form. A <Term a="position">position</Term> $a$ is drawn at <Tex src="y=EC^{-1}a"/>, the point of the population it translates to. The site builds $E$ from the eigenvectors of <Tex src="D^{1/2}CD^{-1/2}"/>; any other choice, such as the triangular one of the Kaleidoscope, turns the picture.

On <Tex src="A_2"/>, <Tex src="A_3"/> and <Tex src="D_4"/> the <Term a="root-population">root populations</Term>, roots for short, form one orbit, that of the highest root: the <G k="types.A2.n_roots"/> roots of <Tex src="A_2"/> make a regular hexagon, the <G k="types.A3.n_roots"/> of <Tex src="A_3"/> a cuboctahedron, and the <G k="types.D4.n_roots"/> of <Tex src="D_4"/> a {'24-cell'}, drawn on three of four coordinates with every root a distinct dot.

<Beat n={2}>

Start from a position with no negative number. A node is **ringed** when its number is positive, that number being its <Term a="ring">ring</Term>. On a board of finite type, free play from the position reaches finitely many positions, its **orbit**, whose convex hull is a polytope; its shape depends only on which nodes are ringed. This is Wythoff's construction <Cite k="Cox35"/>, <Cite k="CKPS95"/>, told here in the language of the game. The corners of a polytope are its **vertices**, the places of a board are its nodes, and here the two words never name the same thing. Rings are numbers, so this module uses the Numbers view.

The orbit has <Tex src="|W|/|W_U|"/> vertices, $W$ being the Weyl group of module five and <Tex src="W_U"/> that of the unringed nodes $U$. Clicking a node rings it or clears its ring; the table under the studio lists what the rings give on <Tex src="A_3"/> and <Tex src="B_3"/>.

<Beat n={4}>

An orbit carries two sets of edges. The **move graph** joins each position $\mu$ to the positions one firing away: what the game does. The **polytope graph** joins vertices that are neighbours in space: its edges are the base edges, from the start $\lambda$ to the position after firing one ringed node, carried along by playing the same moves from both ends. The move graph puts the extra firing after a play, the polytope graph before it.

On the hexagon of <Tex src="A_2"/> from <Tex src="(1,1)"/> each graph has six edges, and four of the edges are shared; the other two edges of the move graph join positions that are not neighbours in space. Showing both, with a switch, is the site's choice.

<Beat n={5}>

The edge from $\lambda$ to the position after firing node $i$ has squared length <Tex src="a_i^2(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=2d_ia_i^2"/>, with $a_i$ the ring and $d_i$ the weight of node $i$, verified by computation on <Tex src="A_3"/>, <Tex src="B_3"/>, <Tex src="C_3"/>, <Tex src="D_4"/>, <Tex src="F_4"/>, <Tex src="G_2"/> and <Tex src="E_6"/>. So a polytope is **uniform**, with all its edges of one length, only when <Tex src="a_i\sqrt{d_i}"/> is the same on every ringed node. On <Tex src="B_3"/> the weights are <G k="types.B3.symmetrizer"/>: nodes {'1'} and {'2'} are long and node {'3'} is short. So the rings <Tex src="(1,1,0)"/>, on the two long nodes, give a uniform truncated octahedron, while <Tex src="(0,1,1)"/> gives a truncated cube, <G k={`${fv('B3', B3_UNIFORM)}[0]`}/> vertices and <G k={`${fv('B3', B3_UNIFORM)}[1]`}/> edges, with squared edge lengths <Tex src="4"/> and <Tex src="2"/>.

The uniform truncated cube needs <Tex src="(0,1,\sqrt2)"/>, which no whole-number position gives. The studio draws whole-number orbits exactly; its **make uniform** is a display option only, replaying the same moves from a multiple of <Tex src="(0,1,\sqrt2)"/> in floating point, and its picture is not an orbit of the game.

<Beat n={6}>

The **Coxeter plane** is spanned by the first and last coordinates of $y$, those of the smallest and largest eigenvalue. Firing every node of one colour class, then every node of the other, the two rounds of the wave of module five, turns this plane by exactly <Tex src="2\pi/h"/>, $h$ being the number of rounds of the wave <Cite k="Cas17"/> ({'Proposition 3.7, Theorem 3.11'}); <Cite k="Ste59"/>. No root lands on the centre, and the roots lie on circles each holding a multiple of $h$ of them, verified by computation on every board of finite type of rank two to eight.

On <Tex src="E_8"/>, where $h$ is <G k="types.E8.coxeter_number"/>, the <G k="types.E8.n_roots"/> roots lie on <G k="types.E8.coxeter_plane.rings" as="count"/> circles of <G k="types.E8.coxeter_number"/>, and all <G k="types.E8.coxeter_plane.distinct_points"/> points are distinct. Each root is joined to the <G k="e8.neighbours_at_inner_product_1"/> roots that have inner product <Tex src="1"/> with it, its nearest neighbours: these <G k={`${fv('E8', E8_ROOT)}[1]`}/> joins are the edges of the root polytope <Tex src="4_{21}"/> <Cite k="Cox73"/>.

A circle can hold more than $h$ roots, and roots can share a point. On <Tex src="D_4"/>, where $h$ is <G k="types.D4.coxeter_number"/>, the <G k="types.D4.n_roots"/> roots lie on <G k="types.D4.coxeter_plane.rings" as="count"/> circles and make only <G k="types.D4.coxeter_plane.distinct_points"/> dots; the studio says so whenever dots are fewer than points.

<LieTheory>

Populations lie in the root lattice and positions are weights in Dynkin labels; a ring is a nonzero Dynkin label of a dominant weight $\lambda$, and the orbit is $W\lambda$. The form is the invariant form $B=DC$, with <Tex src="C_{ij}=\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle"/> as in Kac and SageMath (Bourbaki prints the transpose), and $E$, with <Tex src="E^{\mathsf T}E=B"/>, is a Euclidean embedding of the root lattice. Wythoff's construction is Wythoff's, in Coxeter's account <Cite k="Cox35"/>. The fundamental position $e_i$ is the fundamental weight <Tex src="\omega_i"/>, and the form value is the norm; the orbits of <Tex src="\omega_8"/>, <Tex src="\omega_1"/>, <Tex src="\omega_2"/> of <Tex src="E_8"/> are Coxeter's <Tex src="4_{21}"/>, <Tex src="2_{41}"/>, <Tex src="1_{42}"/> <Cite k="Cox73"/>. The product of the simple reflections of one colour class and then the other is a Coxeter element $c$ <Cite k="Ste59"/>, of order the Coxeter number $h$, with eigenvalues <Tex src="e^{2\pi i m_j/h}"/> for the exponents $m_j$ <Cite k="Cox51"/>; the Coxeter plane is the real plane of the exponents <Tex src="1"/> and <Tex src="h-1"/>, on which $c$ is the rotation through <Tex src="2\pi/h"/> <Cite k="Cas17"/>. Since <Tex src="\det C=1"/>, the root lattice of <Tex src="E_8"/> is its weight lattice; its theta series begins {'1'}, <G k="types.E8.n_roots"/>, <G k="types.E8.fundamental_orbit_sizes[0]"/>, <G k="types.E8.fundamental_orbit_sizes[6]"/>, <Tex src="17\,520"/>.

</LieTheory>
