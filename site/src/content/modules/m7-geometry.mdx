<Beat n={1}>

A population is a list of whole numbers, one for each node, and the form of module two measures it: on a board of finite type every nonzero population has a positive <Term a="form-value">form value</Term>. The form is not the ordinary dot product, so the list is not yet a point you can draw. The cure is a change of coordinates.

Write $B=DC$ for the matrix of the form, with $D$ the diagonal matrix of the <Term a="weights">weights</Term> $d_i$, and find a matrix $E$ with <Tex src="E^{\mathsf T}E=B"/>. The population $p$ is drawn at the point <Tex src="y=Ep"/>, and then <Tex src="y\cdot y'=(p,p')"/>: lengths and angles in the picture are those of the form. A <Term a="position">position</Term> $a$ of the numbers game is drawn at <Tex src="y=EC^{-1}a"/>, the point of the population it translates to. The site builds $E$ from the eigenvectors of <Tex src="D^{1/2}CD^{-1/2}"/>, a symmetric matrix with the eigenvalues of $C$. Any other $E$ with <Tex src="E^{\mathsf T}E=B"/> gives the same picture turned; the Kaleidoscope used the triangular one. Floating point enters only here, and the positions themselves stay exact.

On <Tex src="A_2"/>, <Tex src="A_3"/> and <Tex src="D_4"/> the <Term a="root-population">root populations</Term>, or roots for short, form one orbit, that of the highest root, whose numbers are <G k="types.A2.numbers_of_highest_root"/>, <G k="types.A3.numbers_of_highest_root"/> and <G k="types.D4.numbers_of_highest_root"/>. Drawn as points, the <G k="types.A2.n_roots"/> roots of <Tex src="A_2"/> make a regular hexagon. The <G k="types.A3.n_roots"/> roots of <Tex src="A_3"/> are the corners of a cuboctahedron, with <G k={`${fv('A3', A3_ROOT)}[0]`}/> vertices, <G k={`${fv('A3', A3_ROOT)}[1]`}/> edges and <G k={`${fv('A3', A3_ROOT)}[2]`}/> faces. The <G k="types.D4.n_roots"/> roots of <Tex src="D_4"/> are the corners of a {'24-cell'} in four dimensions, drawn on three of its coordinates, where all of them are distinct dots.

<Beat n={2}>

Start from a position with no negative number, and call node $i$ **ringed** when its number $a_i$ is positive; that number is its <Term a="ring">ring</Term>. Free play of the numbers game from the position reaches finitely many positions, its **orbit**, and the convex hull of their points is a polytope. Its shape depends only on which nodes are ringed, not on the sizes of the rings. This is Wythoff's construction, written in the language of the game <Cite k="Cox35"/>, <Cite k="CKPS95"/>. The corners of a polytope are its **vertices**, the places of a board are its nodes, and on this page the two words never name the same thing. Rings are numbers, so the view of this module is always Numbers.

The orbit has <Tex src="|W|/|W_U|"/> vertices: <Tex src="|W|"/> is the number of elements of the Weyl group of module five, and <Tex src="|W_U|"/> that of the sub-board of the unringed nodes $U$. Clicking a node of the studio rings it or clears its ring. On <Tex src="A_3"/> and <Tex src="B_3"/> the choices of rings give these solids:

<Beat n={3}>

The faces of these polytopes can be counted from the board alone, with no picture and no convex hull. A **face type** is a set $K$ of nodes such that every connected piece of $K$ holds a ringed node. Each face of that type is the orbit polytope of the sub-board $K$, its dimension is the number of nodes in $K$, and there are

<Tex display src="\frac{|W|}{|W_K|\,|W_{K'}|}"/>

of them, where $K'$ is the set of unringed nodes that are neither in $K$ nor joined to $K$. On <Tex src="B_3"/> with rings on nodes {'1'} and {'3'} the table under the figure lists the face types and their numbers: <G k={`${fv('B3', B3_FACES)}[0]`}/> vertices, <G k={`${fv('B3', B3_FACES)}[1]`}/> edges and <G k={`${fv('B3', B3_FACES)}[2]`}/> faces, the counts of the rhombicuboctahedron. The same rule counts the faces of an eight-dimensional polytope: on <Tex src="E_8"/> with one ring on node {'1'} it gives <G k={fv('E8', E8_ONE)}/> faces in the dimensions <Tex src="0"/> to <Tex src="7"/>, the counts of the polytope <Tex src="2_{41}"/>.

<Beat n={4}>

An orbit carries two natural sets of edges. The **move graph** joins each position $\mu$ to the position reached from it by firing any one node: it records what the game does. The **polytope graph** joins the vertices that are neighbours in space. Its edges come from the base edges, from the start $\lambda$ to the position after firing one ringed node, by playing the same moves from both ends. So in the move graph the extra firing comes after a play, and in the polytope graph it comes before the play.

On the hexagon of <Tex src="A_2"/> from <Tex src="(1,1)"/> the two graphs have the same six vertices, each graph has six edges, and four of the edges are shared. The other two edges of the move graph join positions that are not neighbours in space. The studio shows either graph, and the switch between them is the site's choice of how to compare them.

<Beat n={5}>

The edge from the start $\lambda$ to the position after firing node $i$ has squared length <Tex src="a_i^2(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=2d_ia_i^2"/>, where $a_i$ is the ring of node $i$ and $d_i$ its weight from module six. A polytope is **uniform** when all its edges have one length, and for that <Tex src="a_i\sqrt{d_i}"/> must be the same on every ringed node. On <Tex src="B_3"/> the weights are <G k="types.B3.symmetrizer"/>: nodes {'1'} and {'2'} are long and node {'3'} is short. Equal numbers on the two long nodes, <Tex src="(1,1,0)"/>, give a uniform truncated octahedron.

Equal numbers on a long and a short node, <Tex src="(0,1,1)"/>, give the shape of a truncated cube, with <G k={`${fv('B3', B3_UNIFORM)}[0]`}/> vertices and <G k={`${fv('B3', B3_UNIFORM)}[1]`}/> edges, but its edges have squared lengths <Tex src="4"/> and <Tex src="2"/>. The uniform truncated cube needs the numbers <Tex src="(0,1,\sqrt2)"/>, which no whole-number position gives. The studio draws whole-number orbits exactly. Its option **make uniform** is a display option only: it replays the same moves from <Tex src="(0,1,\sqrt2)"/> in floating point, and the picture it draws is not an orbit of the game.

<Beat n={6}>

From rank four on, the studio first draws the roots in one special plane. The **Coxeter plane** is spanned by the first and last coordinates of $y$, those of the smallest and the largest eigenvalue in the recipe of beat one. The game turns it: firing every node of one colour class and then every node of the other, the two rounds of the wave of module five, rotates the plane by exactly <Tex src="2\pi/h"/>, where $h$ is the number of rounds of the wave <Cite k="Cas17"/> ({'Proposition 3.7, Theorem 3.11'}); <Cite k="Ste59"/>. No root lands on the centre. The roots lie on circles about it, each circle holding a multiple of $h$ roots, as verified by computation on every board of rank two to eight.

On <Tex src="E_8"/>, where $h$ is <G k="types.E8.coxeter_number"/>, the <G k="types.E8.n_roots"/> roots lie on <G k="types.E8.coxeter_plane.rings" as="count"/> circles of <G k="types.E8.coxeter_number"/>, and all <G k="types.E8.coxeter_plane.distinct_points"/> points are distinct. Each root is joined to the <G k="e8.neighbours_at_inner_product_1"/> roots that have inner product <Tex src="1"/> with it, its nearest neighbours, and these <G k={`${fv('E8', E8_ROOT)}[1]`}/> joins are the edges of the root polytope <Tex src="4_{21}"/> <Cite k="Cox73"/>.

A circle need not hold exactly $h$ roots, and two roots can land on one point. On <Tex src="D_4"/>, where $h$ is <G k="types.D4.coxeter_number"/>, the <G k="types.D4.n_roots"/> roots lie on <G k="types.D4.coxeter_plane.rings" as="count"/> circles and make only <G k="types.D4.coxeter_plane.distinct_points"/> dots. The studio states the number of distinct dots whenever it is smaller than the number of points. Points coincide on <G k="coxeter_plane_types_with_coincident_points" as="count"/> of the boards of finite type with rank at most eight.

<LieTheory>

Populations are elements of the root lattice and positions are weights written by their Dynkin labels; a ring is a nonzero Dynkin label of a dominant weight $\lambda$, and the orbit is the Weyl group orbit $W\lambda$. The form is the invariant bilinear form $B=DC$, with <Tex src="C_{ij}=\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle"/> as in Kac and SageMath (Bourbaki prints the transpose), and $E$ embeds the root lattice isometrically in Euclidean space. Wythoff's construction of polytopes from a ringed diagram is Wythoff's, in Coxeter's account <Cite k="Cox35"/>. The fundamental position $e_i$ is the fundamental weight <Tex src="\omega_i"/>, and the form value is the norm; the orbits of <Tex src="\omega_8"/>, <Tex src="\omega_1"/> and <Tex src="\omega_2"/> of <Tex src="E_8"/> are Coxeter's polytopes <Tex src="4_{21}"/>, <Tex src="2_{41}"/> and <Tex src="1_{42}"/> <Cite k="Cox73"/>. The product of the simple reflections of one colour class and then of the other is a Coxeter element $c$ <Cite k="Ste59"/>, of order $h$, the Coxeter number. Its eigenvalues are <Tex src="e^{2\pi i m_j/h}"/> for the exponents $m_j$ <Cite k="Cox51"/>, on <Tex src="E_8"/> the numbers <G k="types.E8.exponents" as="plain"/>, and the Coxeter plane is the real plane of the exponents <Tex src="1"/> and <Tex src="h-1"/>, on which $c$ acts as the rotation through <Tex src="2\pi/h"/> <Cite k="Cas17"/>. Since <Tex src="\det C=1"/>, the root lattice of <Tex src="E_8"/> equals its weight lattice, and its theta series begins {'1'}, <G k="types.E8.n_roots"/>, <G k="types.E8.fundamental_orbit_sizes[0]"/>, <G k="types.E8.fundamental_orbit_sizes[6]"/>, <Tex src="17\,520"/>.
