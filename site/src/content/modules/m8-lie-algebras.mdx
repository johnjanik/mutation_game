<Beat n={1}>

This module stays in the <View v="numbers"/> view: positions, their <Term a="numbers">numbers</Term>, and node $i$. The numbers game is due to Mozes <Cite k="Moz90"/>; Proctor used the same firing process before him <Cite k="Pro84"/>, and Eriksson developed it <Cite k="Eri95a"/>.

Fix a <Term a="node">node</Term> $i$ and write $\alpha_i$ for column $i$ of the Cartan matrix $C$. Take a position $\mu$ whose number $\mu_i$ is not negative and subtract $\alpha_i$ from it, $\mu_i$ times. The positions met, <Tex src="\mu,\ \mu-\alpha_i,\ \dots,\ \mu-\mu_i\alpha_i"/>, form the **string** through $\mu$ at node $i$. Firing node $i$ sends $\mu$ to the last of them and turns the whole string end over end. On <Tex src="A_2"/> at node {'1'}, the string through <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A2;node=1].weights_dynkin_labels[0]"/> has two positions, <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A2;node=1].weights_dynkin_labels[0]"/> and <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A2;node=1].weights_dynkin_labels[2]"/>. The string through <Tex src="(2,0)"/> has three, <Tex src="(2,0)"/>, <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A2;node=2].weights_dynkin_labels[1]"/> and <Tex src="(-2,2)"/>, and its middle position is not in the orbit of <Tex src="(2,0)"/>. That orbit holds <Tex src="(2,0)"/>, <Tex src="(-2,2)"/> and <Tex src="(0,-2)"/> only.

In Lie theory the pattern is general: in every finite-dimensional representation of the Lie algebra of the board, its positions on the line through one of them, $\mu$, in the direction of $\alpha_i$ form an unbroken run from <Tex src="\mu-r\alpha_i"/> to <Tex src="\mu+q\alpha_i"/> with <Tex src="r-q=\mu_i"/>, which contains the string of the game. <Unverified item="E10" fallback="This is a standard fact of Lie theory.">See <Cite k="Hum72"/>, sections {'9.4 and 21.3'}.</Unverified>

<Beat n={2}>

Give each <Term a="position">position</Term> of an orbit a basis vector of its own. A **single step** moves a position by $\alpha_i$: the matrix $F_i$ steps down, subtracting $\alpha_i$, and $E_i$ steps up, adding it, and a step that would leave the orbit gives <Tex src="0"/>. A third matrix, $H_i$, counts: it multiplies each position by its number on node $i$.

The smallest case is <Tex src="A_1"/>. The orbit of the <Term a="fundamental-position">fundamental position</Term> <Tex src="e_1"/> has <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A1;node=1].dimension"/> positions, <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A1;node=1].weights_dynkin_labels[0]"/> and <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A1;node=1].weights_dynkin_labels[1]"/>. Here <Tex src="F_1"/> sends <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A1;node=1].weights_dynkin_labels[0]"/> to <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A1;node=1].weights_dynkin_labels[1]"/>, <Tex src="E_1"/> sends it back, and <Tex src="H_1=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)"/>. Firing node {'1'} exchanges them, so on this orbit a whole move is one single step, down or up.

</Beat>

<Beat n={3}>

On <Tex src="A_2"/> the orbit of <Tex src="e_1"/> has <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A2;node=1].dimension"/> positions, <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A2;node=1].weights_dynkin_labels[0]"/>, <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A2;node=1].weights_dynkin_labels[1]"/> and <G k="minuscule_representations[type=A2;node=1].weights_dynkin_labels[2]"/>, so the matrices are <Tex src="{{minuscule_representations[type=A2;node=1].dimension}}\times{{minuscule_representations[type=A2;node=1].dimension}}"/>. The recipe on a position $\mu$: $H_k$ multiplies it by $\mu_k$; $F_k$ gives the position that firing node $k$ reaches if <Tex src="\mu_k=1"/>; $E_k$ gives that position if <Tex src="\mu_k=-1"/>; every other case gives <Tex src="0"/>. A step down subtracts $\alpha_k$, which is the firing move exactly when <Tex src="\mu_k=1"/>, so the game supplies the matrices.

They satisfy the **relations** of Chevalley and Serre, which define the Lie algebra of the board:

<Tex display src="[H_i,H_j]=0,\quad [H_i,E_j]=C_{ij}E_j,\quad [H_i,F_j]=-C_{ij}F_j,\quad [E_i,F_j]=\delta_{ij}H_i,"/>

<Tex display src="\operatorname{ad}(E_i)^{1-C_{ij}}E_j=0,\qquad \operatorname{ad}(F_i)^{1-C_{ij}}F_j=0\quad(i\ne j),"/>

with <Tex src="[X,Y]=XY-YX"/> and <Tex src="\operatorname{ad}(X)Y=[X,Y]"/>. The checker shows both sides of <Tex src="[E_1,F_1]=H_1"/> and finds them equal. Set node $j$ to {'2'}: both sides of <Tex src="[E_1,F_2]=0"/> are the zero matrix.

</Beat>

The same recipe works on bigger orbits: the <G k="minuscule_representations[type=D4;node=1].dimension"/> positions of <Tex src="D_4"/> from node {'1'}, the <G k="minuscule_representations[type=E6;node=1].dimension"/> of <Tex src="E_6"/> from node {'1'} and the <G k="minuscule_representations[type=E7;node=7].dimension"/> of <Tex src="E_7"/> from node {'7'}. At any other node some position $\mu$ has a number $\mu_k$ of size at least <Tex src="2"/>; no single step acts on it, so <Tex src="[E_k,F_k]"/> sends it to <Tex src="0"/> while $H_k$ does not, and the recipe fails. <Beat n={5}>

On a minuscule orbit, firing node $k$ permutes the positions, so its matrix holds a single <Tex src="1"/> in each column: it is <Tex src="E_k+F_k+P_k^0"/>, where <Tex src="P_k^0"/> keeps each position whose number on node $k$ is <Tex src="0"/> and sends the others to <Tex src="0"/>. Lie theory turns single steps into elements of a group by exponentials, and here <Tex src="\exp(E_k)\exp(-F_k)\exp(E_k)=E_k-F_k+P_k^0"/>, the **signed move**: the move again, with one sign changed in each pair of exchanged positions. Its square is <Tex src="(-1)^{H_k}"/>, which multiplies each position by <Tex src="-1"/> raised to its number on node $k$. Both were verified by computation at every node of the representations of beat {'4'}. So on a minuscule orbit a move of the game is an element of the Lie group with its signs dropped, and half a move is a single step.

<aside id="quiver-representations" aria-labelledby="quiver-representations-title" data-sidebar="quivers" style={{ borderInlineStart: '4px solid var(--sign-zero-outline)', paddingInlineStart: '16px', marginBlock: '24px' }}>
<h2 id="quiver-representations-title">Sidebar: reflection functors and Gabriel's theorem</h2>
<p>Keep one of the two roads of each line of a simply laced <Term a="board">board</Term> (choose a direction for each line), and the result is a <strong>quiver</strong>, with vertices and arrows. A representation of the quiver puts a vector space at each vertex and a linear map on each arrow. At a vertex where every arrow points in, a reflection functor replaces the space there and turns the arrows at the vertex round. For an indecomposable representation other than the simple one at that vertex, the dimension there changes exactly as the population of a city changes when that <Term a="city">city</Term> mutates <Cite k="BGP73"/> ({'Theorem 1.1'}). Gabriel's theorem: when the underlying board is of type <Tex src="A"/>, <Tex src="D"/> or <Tex src="E"/>, the indecomposable representations correspond to the positive <Term a="root-population">root populations</Term> (<Cite k="BGP73"/>, {'Theorem 3.1'}; first proved in <Cite k="Gab72"/>).</p>
<p>A quiver arrow is an orientation, and a quiver gives only symmetric Cartan matrices. An arrowhead on a double or triple line of this site says something else: more <Term a="roads">roads</Term> run one way than the other, and the roots differ in length.</p>
</aside>

<LieTheory>

The matrices <Tex src="E_i,F_i,H_i"/> are Chevalley generators of the simple Lie algebra of the board, and the relations of beat {'3'} are the Chevalley-Serre relations, written with <Tex src="C_{ij}=\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle"/> as in Kac and SageMath; Bourbaki prints the transpose. A position is a weight in Dynkin labels, $e_i$ is the fundamental weight <Tex src="\omega_i"/>, and the string of the game is part of the <Tex src="\alpha_i"/>-string through $\mu$, with <Tex src="r-q=\langle\mu,\alpha_i^\vee\rangle"/>. A <Term a="minuscule-node">minuscule node</Term> gives a minuscule weight: its Weyl group orbit carries a representation in which each weight occurs once. The signed move lifts the simple reflection <Tex src="s_k"/> to the group, with square <Tex src="(-1)^{H_k}"/>. Wildberger builds these representations on the ideals of a minuscule <Term a="heap">heap</Term> <Cite k="W03a"/>, with proofs in <Cite k="Gre13"/>, and <Cite k="W03c"/> builds <Tex src="G_2"/> combinatorially in another basis, with entries from <Tex src="-2"/> to <Tex src="2"/>. The smallest nontrivial representation of <Tex src="E_8"/>, by a standard fact, is its adjoint representation.

</LieTheory>
