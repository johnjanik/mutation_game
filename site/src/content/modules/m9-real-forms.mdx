<Beat n={1}>

A complex Lie algebra can be cut down to a real one in several ways, each a **real form** with an **involution** $\theta$: a move that, done twice, gives back the start.

The board <Tex src="A_1"/> has one node. Left **white**, it stands for <Tex src="\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})"/>: $\theta$ turns every Martian of the <Term a="population">population</Term> into an anti-Martian, <Tex src="{{types.A1.positive_roots[0]}}"/> into <Tex src={tx(A1_WHITE)}/>. Painted **black**, it stands for <Tex src="\mathfrak{su}(2)"/>: $\theta$ first plays a <Term a="mutate">mutation</Term>, giving <Tex src={tx(A1_MUTATED)}/>, then changes every sign, giving back <Tex src={tx(A1_BLACK)}/>. A <Term a="node">node</Term> is black exactly when $\theta$ fixes its root, for every real form, verified by computation; a <Term a="real-form">real form</Term> whose $\theta$ fixes every root is **compact**. The data of beat {'5'} differ too: real rank <G k={`${S('A1', 'sl(2,R)')}.real_rank`}/> against <G k={`${S('A1', 'su(2)')}.real_rank`}/>, <Tex src="\dim\mathfrak k"/> <G k={`${S('A1', 'sl(2,R)')}.dim_k`}/> against <G k={`${S('A1', 'su(2)')}.dim_k`}/>. These are the <G k="admissible_counts.A1.real_forms_including_compact"/> real forms of <Tex src="A_1"/>.

<Beat n={2}>

A **Satake diagram** on a <Term a="board">board</Term> of finite type is a pair <Tex src="(X,\tau)"/>: a set $X$ of <Term a="black">black</Term> nodes, the rest <Term a="white">white</Term>, and a symmetry $\tau$ of the board with <Tex src="\tau^2=1"/> and <Tex src="\tau(X)=X"/>; white nodes exchanged by $\tau$ are joined by an **arrow**. Its <Term a="involution">involution</Term> is <Tex src="\theta=-\,w_X\circ\tau"/>, where $w_X$ is the longest element of the black sub-board.

So $\theta$ is played in three stages: move each city's <Term a="population">population</Term> along the arrows of $\tau$; play the word of $w_X$, recorded by the numbers game as the nodes fired from all ones on the black sub-board, the lowest positive node first; turn every Martian into an anti-Martian and back, as in Wildberger's Mars story <Cite k="W20"/>. The <Term a="numbers-game">numbers game</Term> is Mozes' <Cite k="Moz90"/>, with Proctor's earlier use of the same process <Cite k="Pro84"/> and Eriksson's development <Cite k="Eri95a"/>.

Paint nodes {'1'} and {'3'} of <Tex src="A_3"/> black: <Tex src="\mathfrak{su}^*(4)"/>, also written <Tex src="\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{H})"/>, whose $w_X$ has the <Term a="word">word</Term> <G k={`${S('A3', 'su*(4)')}.w_X_word`} as="word"/>. From <Tex src="{{types.A3.positive_roots[1]}}"/>, one Martian in city {'2'}, stage one changes nothing, stage two mutates <Term a="city">city</Term> {W2_LABELS[0]}, then city {W2_LABELS[1]}, giving <Tex src={tx(L(SU4, ST2[0]))}/> and <Tex src={tx(L(SU4, ST2[1]))}/>, and stage three gives <Tex src={tx(L(SU4, ST3))}/>.

<ol data-conditions>
<li data-condition="1"><Tex src="\tau^2=1"/> and <Tex src="\tau(X)=X"/>;</li>
<li data-condition="2">on the black nodes, $\tau$ agrees with <Tex src="-w_X"/>: <Tex src="w_X(\alpha_i)=-\alpha_{\tau(i)}"/> for every black node $i$;</li>

</ol>
