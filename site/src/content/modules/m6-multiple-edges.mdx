On <Tex src="B_2"/> the builder rejects the equal weights <Tex src="2,2,2"/> that pass on <Tex src="A_2"/>, and accepts exactly those where city {'1'} weighs twice city {'2'} and the road as much as city {'1'}, such as <Tex src="4,2,4"/>.

Half the city weights, <G k="types.B2.symmetrizer"/>, are the <Term a="weights">weights</Term> $d_i$. A board with positive weights such that $d_iA_{ji}=d_jA_{ij}$ is a <Term a="balanced-board">balanced board</Term>, and city $i$ has squared length <Tex src="(\alpha_i,\alpha_i)=2d_i"/>: <Tex src="4"/> and <Tex src="2"/> here. As $d_i/d_j=A_{ij}/A_{ji}$, more roads run from the long city to the short one. On <Tex src="G_2"/>, with weights <G k="types.G2.symmetrizer"/>, city {'2'} is long, with squared length <Tex src="6"/>: the ratio of squared lengths is <G k="types.G2.long_to_short_ratio"/>, against <G k="types.B2.long_to_short_ratio"/> on <Tex src="B_2"/>. On <Tex src="F_4"/> cities {'1'}, {'2'} are long and {'3'}, {'4'} short.

Exactly the balanced boards have a form with positive city weights that no mutation changes, unique up to a factor on a connected board <Cite k="W20"/> ({'Theorems 8.4, 8.7'}). Wildberger's definition, a condition on cycles, agrees on boards with roads both ways <Cite k="W20"/> ({'Corollary 8.5'}). A lone one-way road, lacking positive weights, is balanced only in his sense.

The numbers game is Mozes' <Cite k="Moz90"/>, with Proctor's earlier use of the same process <Cite k="Pro84"/> and Eriksson's development <Cite k="Eri95b"/>. With single roads the two moves are transposes. In general it is the mutation game on the **reversed board**, every road turned round, transposed: <Tex src="R_i(C)=S_i(C^{\mathsf T})^{\mathsf T}"/>, verified by computation. Reversing <Tex src="B_2"/> gives <Tex src="C_2"/>, the same board renamed: <Tex src="B_2=C_2"/>. For <Tex src="n\ge 3"/>, <Tex src="B_n"/> and <Tex src="C_n"/> differ, each the reversal of the other; <Tex src="F_4"/> and <Tex src="G_2"/> reversed are themselves, long and short exchanged.

On <Tex src="B_2"/> the complete symbolic game fires <Tex src="x_1"/>, <Tex src="2x_1+x_2"/>, <Tex src="x_1+x_2"/>, <Tex src="x_2"/>: the positive roots of the reversed board <Cite k="Don08"/> ({'Theorem 5.2'}), whose highest root is <G k="types.B2.highest_root_of_reversed_board"/> where <Tex src="B_2"/> has <G k="types.B2.highest_root"/>. <Beat n={5}>

A **symmetry** of a board with single roads renumbers its nodes and keeps every road; on <Tex src="A_3"/> it exchanges nodes {'1'} and {'3'}, with **orbits**, the sets of nodes it permutes, <Tex src="\{1,3\}"/> and <Tex src="\{2\}"/>. If no orbit holds two joined nodes, the <Term a="folded-board">folded board</Term> has one node per orbit, with as many roads from orbit $O'$ to orbit $O$ as run from the nodes of $O'$ into one node of $O$: **folding the board**, by orbit sums.

Firing all nodes of an orbit is a **symmetric move**: <Tex src="(1,1,1)"/> becomes <Tex src="(-1,3,-1)"/>, and on the folded board <G k={`${A3F}.folds_to`}/> firing node {'1'} turns <Tex src="(1,1)"/> into <Tex src="(-1,3)"/>. Symmetric play is play on the folded board, in both games, with the same numbers, verified by computation on <Tex src="200"/> random plays per fold. Larger orbits become longer roots. The rotation <Tex src="1\to3\to4\to1"/> folds <Tex src="D_4"/> to <G k={`${D4R}.folds_to`}/> and the flip of <Tex src="E_6"/> to <G k={`${E6F}.folds_to`}/>; <Tex src="A_{2n-1}"/> folds to <Tex src="B_n"/> and <Tex src="D_{n+1}"/> to <Tex src="C_n"/>, while on <Tex src="A_{2n}"/> the middle orbit holds a road and the flip does not fold.

<Beat n={6}>

The other construction called folding averages every root population over the orbits. On <Tex src="A_3"/> one Martian in city {'1'} and one in city {'3'} both average to half a Martian in each. The averages form a root system of the dual type, larger orbits giving shorter roots: **restricting the roots**, by orbit averages.

On <Tex src="A_5"/> folding gives <G k={`${A5F}.folds_to`}/>, averaging <G k={`${A5F}.averaged_roots_type`}/>; whenever the board folds, the two Cartan matrices are transposes, verified by computation. On <Tex src="A_4"/> the flip does not fold, yet averaging gives the non-reduced system <G k={`${A4F}.averaged_roots_type`}/>. In general <Tex src="A_{2n-1}"/> averages to <Tex src="C_n"/>, <Tex src="D_{n+1}"/> to <Tex src="B_n"/>, <Tex src="A_{2n}"/> to <Tex src="BC_n"/>; <Tex src="E_6"/> and <Tex src="D_4"/> give <Tex src="F_4"/> and <Tex src="G_2"/> with long and short exchanged. In rank two <Tex src="B_2=C_2"/>, so <Tex src="A_3"/> gives <G k={`${A3F}.averaged_roots_type`}/> both ways. Module nine returns to this construction.

<LieTheory>

The arrow of a Dynkin diagram points from the long simple root to the short one. Here $C_{ij}=\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle$, as in Kac and SageMath; Bourbaki prints the transpose. Reversing the roads transposes $C$, giving the dual root system of coroots; the numbers fired from $\rho$ are heights of positive coroots. Folding the board is the folding of Lusztig and Stembridge, whose Weyl group is the subgroup of $W$ commuting with $\sigma$ (<Cite k="Ste08"/>; <Cite k="Lus93"/>), and gives the orbit Lie algebra of <Cite k="FSS96"/>. Restricting the roots gives, for <Tex src="A_{2n-1}"/>, <Tex src="D_{n+1}"/>, <Tex src="E_6"/> and the rotation of <Tex src="D_4"/>, the roots of the fixed subalgebra <Cite k="FSS96"/>. <Unverified item="E16" fallback="The case of A with an even index is left aside here.">For <Tex src="A_{2n}"/> the fixed subalgebra is <Tex src="\mathfrak{so}(2n+1)"/>, of type <Tex src="B_n"/>, using only part of <Tex src="BC_n"/>.</Unverified> For a symmetry of order two it gives the restricted roots of the quasi-split real form, whose Satake diagram is all white with arrows $\sigma$: verified by computation.
