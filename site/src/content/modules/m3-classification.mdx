<Beat n={1}>

In module one the Orbit Garden stopped by itself on <Tex src="A_3"/>, <Tex src="D_4"/> and <Tex src="E_8"/>, and kept growing on the triangle. Say that a board **closes up** when it has only finitely many root populations. The **Classification Lab** below asks which boards close up, for any board you draw: add cities, set the roads between two of them, and the lab grows the root populations layer by layer, as the garden did.

It opens on the path <Tex src="A_3"/>, where the count stops by itself at <G k="types.A3.n_roots"/> root populations, all of them. Its picker also holds the other boards of this lesson: the fork <Tex src="D_4"/>, with <G k="types.D4.n_roots"/>; the tree <Tex src="E_6"/>, with <G k="types.E6.n_roots"/>; the triangle, listed as <Tex src="\tilde A_2"/>; and the **star with four arms**, a centre city joined to four others, listed as <Tex src="\tilde D_4"/>. On the triangle the count does not stop by itself. The lab halts it after <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=triangle].layers"/> layers, holding <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=triangle].root_populations_found"/> root populations with <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=triangle].new_per_layer[1]"/> new ones in every layer after the first, and says that it stopped. The star halts at the same bound. A halt marks where the search was cut off; it says nothing about the board.

Draw boards of your own. Among connected simple graphs, the ones that close up are the paths, the forks (a path whose last city splits into two), and three special trees, each a centre city with three arms of one city, two cities, and two, three or four cities; every other connected simple graph runs away.

<Beat n={2}>

The reason is the form of module two. On a simple graph the **form value** of a population $p$ is

<Tex src="(p,p)=2\sum_i p_i^2-2\sum_{\text{roads } ij} p_ip_j," display/>

where the second sum has one term for each pair of joined cities, and no mutation changes it. The form is **positive definite** when every population except the empty one has a positive form value. On <Tex src="A_3"/> it is: the Form Finder beside the verdict gives each single Martian the value <Tex src="2"/>. On the triangle it is not, since <Tex src="(1,1,1)"/> has the value <Tex src="6-6=0"/>.

The **spectral radius** of a board is the largest eigenvalue of its matrix of road counts. You need not compute it: the verdict panel shows it to four decimals, below <Tex src="2"/> on <Tex src="A_3"/> and exactly <Tex src="2"/> on the triangle and on the star.

A board is **balanced** when its cities can be weighted so that such a form exists; every simple graph is balanced, with every weight equal to one. Wildberger proves that on a balanced board three things go together: the game closes up exactly when the form is positive definite, and exactly when the spectral radius is below <Tex src="2"/> <Cite k="W20"/> ({'Theorems 4.1 and 8.8'}). So the simple graphs that close up are exactly <Tex src="A_n"/>, <Tex src="D_n"/>, <Tex src="E_6"/>, <Tex src="E_7"/> and <Tex src="E_8"/>. Boards with double or triple roads add <Tex src="B_n"/>, <Tex src="C_n"/>, <Tex src="F_4"/> and <Tex src="G_2"/> to the boards of finite type, whose forms are positive definite too; they arrive in module six.

<Beat n={3}>

The boards of spectral radius exactly <Tex src="2"/> sit on the boundary, and the site calls them **affine**; Wildberger lists the connected ones with roads both ways <Cite k="W20"/> ({'Theorem 4.1'}). Each carries a **frozen population**, a population that no mutation changes. A mutation leaves a city as it is when its neighbours send in exactly twice what it holds, so on a simple graph a frozen population is one in which twice each city equals the sum of its neighbours.

On a cycle every city has two neighbours, so one Martian in every city will do. On the square, the cycle of four cities, the frozen population is <G k="affine.A3.marks"/>, and the lab gives its spectral radius as <G k="classification_tests[label=square (the 4-cycle)].spectral_radius_4_decimals"/>. On the star the centre has four neighbours, so it must hold twice what an arm holds: <G k="affine.D4.marks[2]"/> Martians in the centre and one in each arm. With the cities numbered from <Tex src="0"/> and the centre as city <Tex src="2"/>, that is <G k="affine.D4.marks"/>. Wildberger draws every affine board with this population <Cite k="W20"/>, and Proctor uses the same condition in his classification puzzles <Cite k="Pro93"/>. In both figures every city is happy; that every mutation fixes the frozen population is also verified by computation.

<Beat n={4}>

A frozen population is not a root population: no mutation moves it, so no play from a single Martian can arrive at it. Add one Martian to it, though, and you have a root population again. The board below starts from the frozen population of the star with one more Martian in city <Tex src="0"/>, that is <Tex src="(2,1,2,1,1)"/>. Mutating cities <Tex src="1"/>, <Tex src="3"/> or <Tex src="4"/> changes nothing there; cities <Tex src="0"/> and <Tex src="2"/> lead on to other root populations.

Wildberger shows that on every affine board one Martian in a suitable city, added to the frozen population, gives a root population <Cite k="W20"/> ({'Proposition 6.1'}). On the boards of this lesson every city is suitable, as verified by computation. The frozen population can then be added again and again, so the root populations grow without end.

The garden beside the board grows the root populations of the star for <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=star with four arms].layers"/> layers. It finds <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=star with four arms].root_populations_found"/>, and the new ones per layer, from the first, are <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=star with four arms].new_per_layer"/>: the count never settles at zero. The garden reports that it stopped at its bound, and the verdict beside that report, affine, comes from the classification of the board, not from the count.

<Beat n={5}>

The boundary boards can be built from the finite ones with Kostant's game, attributed to Kostant and met in module one: start from one Martian and make only raising moves until no city is unhappy. On <Tex src="D_4"/> from city <Tex src="1"/> the play passes through <Tex src="(1,1,0,0)"/>, <Tex src="(1,1,1,0)"/> and <Tex src="(1,1,1,1)"/> and ends at <G k="kostant.D4.end_from_node[0]"/> after <G k="kostant.D4.moves_from_node[0]"/> moves, which is <Tex src="h-2"/> for the Coxeter number <Tex src="h={{types.D4.coxeter_number}}"/>.

At the end, look at what a mutation would do to each city. The amount by which it would lower a city is that city's number, and the numbers are <G k="kostant.D4.final_numbers_from_node[0]"/>: only city <Tex src="2"/>, the centre, would drop, and every other city is happy. Now join a new city, by one road each way, to every city that ends with a positive number. Here that is the centre alone, and the new board is a centre with four arms: the star of beat three. On a board with single roads this recipe gives the affine board from every board of finite type and every starting city, as verified by computation. When the play is over, the tool names the new board and offers a link, add the city, that opens it in the lab.

<Beat n={6}>

The **Mutation Conjecture** is Wildberger's <Cite k="W20"/> ({'p. 57'}): on any multigraph, every root population consists entirely of Martians or entirely of anti-Martians. His unpublished manuscript poses it for simple graphs as the Mutation Problem <Cite k="W-MS"/> ({'p. 2'}), and an earlier paper records it for simple graphs with the remark that no direct combinatorial proof is known <Cite k="W03a"/>.

Read literally, with one-way roads allowed, the statement is false. Take two cities and a single road from city <Tex src="1"/> to city <Tex src="2"/>, with no road back. From <G k="one_way_counterexample.start"/>, mutate city <Tex src="2"/> and then city <Tex src="1"/>: the population becomes <Tex src="(1,1)"/> and then <G k="one_way_counterexample.result"/>, Martians and anti-Martians side by side, as verified by computation. The lab below has its conjecture hunter switched on and finds exactly this. The theorems of the earlier beats assume roads in both directions, and reading the conjecture for such boards is this site's interpretation.

<Unverified item="E4" fallback="On every board with two-way roads that the script checking this site has tried, no root population mixes Martians and anti-Martians: every board of finite type, and the first 2500 or so root populations of every untwisted affine board of rank at most 8 and of ten twisted affine boards.">For such boards the statement is true: a root population is a real root of the Kac-Moody algebra of the board, and in Kac's theory every root of such an algebra is a combination of simple roots with coefficients all of one sign <Cite k="Kac90"/>.</Unverified> What remains open is a proof that uses only the game: a reason, in cities and roads, why the two kinds never mix. <OpenProblemLink>The open problem page</OpenProblemLink> collects what is known and how to send an attempt.

On a board of your own with roads both ways, the hunter reports that it found no mixed population among the root populations it grew. That is the report of a finite search, not a proof.

</Beat>

<LieTheory>

A <Term a="board">board</Term> is a generalized Cartan matrix <Tex src="C"/>, in the convention <Tex src="C_{ij}=\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle"/>; Bourbaki prints its transpose. The site's <Term a="finite">finite</Term>, <Term a="affine">affine</Term> and <Term a="neither">neither</Term> are the finite, affine and indefinite types, a <Term a="balanced-board">balanced</Term> board is a symmetrizable matrix, and on a simple graph the form is the Tits form. <Term a="root-population">Root populations</Term> are the real roots. The <Term a="frozen-population">frozen population</Term> is the null root <Tex src="\delta"/>, whose entries are the marks; its nonzero multiples are imaginary roots and are never root populations. <Term a="kostants-game">Kostant's game</Term> climbs to the highest root <Tex src="\theta"/>, and the new city of the recipe carries <Tex src="\alpha_0=\delta-\theta"/>, which gives the untwisted affine diagram <Tex src="\tilde X_n"/>, also written <Tex src="X_n^{(1)}"/>. The comarks weight the level <Tex src="\langle\lambda,K\rangle"/> of a weight, which module four uses.

</LieTheory>
