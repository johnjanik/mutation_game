<Beat n={1}>

In module one the Orbit Garden stopped by itself on <Tex src="A_3"/>, <Tex src="D_4"/> and <Tex src="E_8"/> and kept growing on the triangle. Say that a board **closes up** when it has finitely many root populations. The **Classification Lab** grows the root populations of any board you draw, layer by layer, as the garden did.

It opens on the path <Tex src="A_3"/>, where the count stops by itself at <G k="types.A3.n_roots"/>. Its picker holds the other boards of this lesson: the fork <Tex src="D_4"/>, with <G k="types.D4.n_roots"/>; the tree <Tex src="E_6"/>, with <G k="types.E6.n_roots"/>; the triangle, <Tex src="\tilde A_2"/>; and the **star with four arms**, a centre city joined to four others, <Tex src="\tilde D_4"/>. On the triangle the lab halts the count after <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=triangle].layers"/> layers, holding <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=triangle].root_populations_found"/> root populations, <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=triangle].new_per_layer[1]"/> new in every layer after the first, and says that it stopped; the star halts at the same bound. A halt marks where the search was cut off, nothing more.

Draw boards of your own. Among connected simple graphs, the ones that close up are the paths, the forks (a path whose last city splits in two) and three special trees, a centre city with arms of one city, two cities, and two, three or four cities; every other connected simple graph runs away.

<Beat n={2}>

The reason is the form of module two. On a simple graph the **form value** of a population $p$ is

<Tex src="(p,p)=2\sum_i p_i^2-2\sum_{\text{roads } ij} p_ip_j," display/>

with one term in the second sum for each pair of joined cities; no mutation changes it. The form is **positive definite** when every population except the empty one has a positive value. On <Tex src="A_3"/> it is, and the Form Finder gives each single Martian the value <Tex src="2"/>. On the triangle it is not: <Tex src="(1,1,1)"/> has the value <Tex src="6-6=0"/>.

The **spectral radius** of a board is the largest eigenvalue of its matrix of road counts. The verdict panel shows it to four decimals: below <Tex src="2"/> on <Tex src="A_3"/>, exactly <Tex src="2"/> on the triangle and the star.

A board is **balanced** when its cities can be weighted so that such a form exists; every simple graph is balanced, with all weights one. Wildberger proves that on a balanced board the game closes up exactly when the form is positive definite, and exactly when the spectral radius is below <Tex src="2"/> <Cite k="W20"/> ({'Theorems 4.1 and 8.8'}). So the simple graphs that close up are exactly <Tex src="A_n"/>, <Tex src="D_n"/>, <Tex src="E_6"/>, <Tex src="E_7"/> and <Tex src="E_8"/>. Boards with double or triple roads add <Tex src="B_n"/>, <Tex src="C_n"/>, <Tex src="F_4"/> and <Tex src="G_2"/>, whose forms are positive definite too; they arrive in module six.

<Beat n={3}>

The boards of spectral radius exactly <Tex src="2"/> sit on the boundary; the site calls them **affine**, and Wildberger lists the connected ones with roads both ways <Cite k="W20"/> ({'Theorem 4.1'}). Each carries a **frozen population**, one that no mutation changes. A mutation leaves a city as it is when its neighbours send in exactly twice what it holds, so on a simple graph a frozen population is one in which twice each city equals the sum of its neighbours.

On the square, the cycle of four cities, the frozen population is <G k="affine.A3.marks"/>, and the lab prints its spectral radius as <G k="classification_tests[label=square (the 4-cycle)].spectral_radius_4_decimals"/>. On the star the centre has four neighbours, so it holds <G k="affine.D4.marks[2]"/> and each arm one: with the cities numbered from <Tex src="0"/> and the centre as city <Tex src="2"/>, <G k="affine.D4.marks"/>. Wildberger draws each affine board with this population <Cite k="W20"/>; Proctor builds classification puzzles on the same condition <Cite k="Pro93"/>. In both figures every city is happy.

<Beat n={4}>

A frozen population is not a root population: no mutation moves it, so no play from a single Martian arrives at it. Add one Martian, though, and you have a root population again. The board below starts from the frozen population of the star plus one Martian in city <Tex src="0"/>. Mutating cities <Tex src="1"/>, <Tex src="3"/> or <Tex src="4"/> changes nothing; cities <Tex src="0"/> and <Tex src="2"/> lead on.

Wildberger shows that on every affine board one Martian in a suitable city, added to the frozen population, gives a root population <Cite k="W20"/> ({'Proposition 6.1'}); on the boards of this lesson every city is suitable, as verified by computation. The frozen population can be added again and again, so the root populations never end. The garden beside the board grows them on the star for <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=star with four arms].layers"/> layers and finds <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=star with four arms].root_populations_found"/>, new ones in every layer: <G k="layer_growth_examples[label=star with four arms].new_per_layer"/>. Its verdict, affine, comes from classifying the board, not from the count.

<Beat n={5}>

The boundary boards can be built from the finite ones with Kostant's game, attributed to Kostant and met in module one: from one Martian, make raising moves until no city is unhappy. On <Tex src="D_4"/> from city <Tex src="1"/> the play passes through <Tex src="(1,1,0,0)"/>, <Tex src="(1,1,1,0)"/> and <Tex src="(1,1,1,1)"/> to <G k="kostant.D4.end_from_node[0]"/>, in <G k="kostant.D4.moves_from_node[0]"/> moves.

At the end, each city's number is the amount by which a mutation would lower it. The numbers are <G k="kostant.D4.final_numbers_from_node[0]"/>: only city <Tex src="2"/>, the centre, would drop. Join a new city, by one road each way, to every city that ends with a positive number. Here that is the centre alone, and the new board is the star of beat three. On every board of finite type with single roads and rank at most eight, this recipe gives the affine board from every starting city, as verified by computation. At the end the tool names the new board, with a link, add the city, that opens it in the lab.

<Beat n={6}>

The **Mutation Conjecture** is Wildberger's <Cite k="W20"/> ({'p. 57'}): on any multigraph, every root population consists entirely of Martians or entirely of anti-Martians. His unpublished manuscript poses it for simple graphs <Cite k="W-MS"/> ({'p. 2'}), and an earlier paper records it for simple graphs as a proposition, noting that no direct combinatorial proof is known <Cite k="W03a"/>.

Read literally, with one-way roads allowed, the statement is false. Take a single road from city <Tex src="1"/> to city <Tex src="2"/> and none back. From <G k="one_way_counterexample.start"/>, mutate city <Tex src="2"/>, then city <Tex src="1"/>: the population becomes <Tex src="(1,1)"/>, then <G k="one_way_counterexample.result"/>, as verified by computation. The hunter in the lab below finds exactly this. The theorems above assume roads both ways; reading the conjecture for such boards is this site's interpretation.

<Unverified item="E4" fallback="On every board with two-way roads that the script checking this site has tried, no root population mixes Martians and anti-Martians: every board of finite type of rank at most 8, and the first 2500 or so root populations of every untwisted affine board of rank at most 8 and of ten twisted affine boards.">For such boards the statement is true: a root population is a real root of the Kac-Moody algebra of the board, and in Kac's theory every root of such an algebra is a combination of simple roots with coefficients all non-negative or all non-positive <Cite k="Kac90"/>.</Unverified> What remains open is a proof by the game alone, a reason in cities and roads why the two kinds never mix; <OpenProblemLink>the open problem page</OpenProblemLink> says what is known and how to send an attempt.

On your own boards with roads both ways the hunter reports none among the root populations it grew: the report of a finite search, not a proof.

<LieTheory>

A <Term a="board">board</Term> is a generalized Cartan matrix <Tex src="C"/>, in the convention <Tex src="C_{ij}=\langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle"/>; Bourbaki prints its transpose. <Term a="finite">Finite</Term>, <Term a="affine">affine</Term> and <Term a="neither">neither</Term> are the finite, affine and indefinite types; <Term a="balanced-board">balanced</Term> is symmetrizable; on a simple graph the form is the Tits form. <Term a="root-population">Root populations</Term> are the real roots. The <Term a="frozen-population">frozen population</Term> is the null root <Tex src="\delta"/>, whose entries are the marks; its nonzero multiples are imaginary roots, never root populations. <Term a="kostants-game">Kostant's game</Term> ends at the highest root <Tex src="\theta"/>, and the new city carries <Tex src="\alpha_0=\delta-\theta"/>, giving the untwisted affine diagram <Tex src="\tilde X_n"/>, also written <Tex src="X_n^{(1)}"/>. The comarks weight the level <Tex src="\langle\lambda,K\rangle"/>, which module four uses.

</LieTheory>
