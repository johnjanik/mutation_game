## 11. Sources

### 11.0 How to read this section

Each entry gives the citation, where a copy can be read, a status, and its role on the site. Entries whose keys are not cited in `SPEC.md` are background for the history and sources pages; a few items (seminar abstracts, expository posts, software) have no key because nothing cites them by key. Status uses the labels of `SPEC.md` section 0.3: [S] read in the source itself during this research; [S2] reported by a later source that was read; [V] checked by `reference/verify.py`; [U] unverified. "Read" means that the text was obtained and read by the research tools; some texts were read through a tool that returns an extraction of a web page or PDF, so wording, formula numbers and page numbers taken from them should be compared with the original before they are quoted.

**Limits of the research.** Three kinds of limit shaped this record, and each entry says when one applies.

1. *Hosts that refuse automated reading.* ScienceDirect (Mozes; Eriksson's journal papers; Proctor 1984 and 1999; the published versions of two of Wildberger's 2003 papers), Project Euclid (Araki), JSTOR (Satake; Kostant), Springer and Cambridge book pages, MathOverflow, and YouTube watch pages. Papers on those hosts were confirmed as citations and described from later papers that cite them.
2. *Books.* Bourbaki, Humphreys' Lie algebra text, Kac's book, Helgason, Fulton and Harris, Carter, Coxeter's *Regular Polytopes* and Conway and Sloane were not opened. Section and theorem numbers attributed to them are from memory and marked [U].
3. *Search allowance.* The web-search allowance for this research ran out before three questions were settled: the list and dates of Wildberger's video lectures; whether anyone has connected Satake diagrams with node-firing games; and further interactive prior art. A follow-up request can continue these searches.

What was read in full: both of Wildberger's texts on the mutation game (the 2020 paper and the manuscript, from the project files), his $G_2$ paper and the preprints of the two other 2003 papers; Donnelly's and Donnelly and Eriksson's papers; Eriksson's 1995 paper in the Journal of Algebraic Combinatorics; Gashi, Schedler and Speyer; Kolb, Dobson and Kolb, and Regelskis and Vlaar on Satake diagrams (arXiv versions); Steinberg 1959; Fomin and Zelevinsky; Stembridge; Bernstein, Gelfand and Ponomarev; and the software sources named below.

### 11.1 The mutation game (Wildberger) and work derived from it

**[W20]** N. J. Wildberger, "The Mutation Game, Coxeter-Dynkin Graphs, and Generalized Root Systems", *Algebra Colloquium* 27 (2020), no. 1, 55-78. DOI [10.1142/S1005386720000061](https://doi.org/10.1142/S1005386720000061). MSC 05C20, 20F55, 17B22.

- Access: closed (World Scientific); no open copy was found. The PDF is in the project as `S1005386720000061.pdf` and was **read in full** for this specification [S].
- Contents by section: 1 Introduction: ADE graphs. 2 The Mutation Game on a Directed Graph (the Mars story; a three-city example with populations 5, 4, 7; the Mutation Conjecture, unnumbered, p. 57). 3 Generalized Root Systems and Dynkin Diagrams (root systems over a general bilinear form). 4 Directed Multigraphs (edge "type $(m,n)$"; adjacency matrix; spectral radius; **Theorem 4.1**: for a connected bidirected multigraph without loops, spectral radius 2 exactly for List A and below 2 exactly for List B; Corollary 4.3). 5 Mutation-Reflections on Directed Multigraphs (the formula for $s_x$; **Theorem 5.1**: $s_x^2 = 1$, non-neighbours commute, and $s_xs_y$ has finite order above 2 exactly for edge types $(1,1)$, $(1,2)$ or $(2,1)$, $(1,3)$ or $(3,1)$, with orders 3, 4, 6; definitions of simple root, root population, $R(X)$, $R^{\pm}(X)$). 6 Multigraphs with Finite Root Populations (Proposition 6.1 and List A2: explicit mutation words that add the Perron-Frobenius population $\alpha_0$ to a simple root on every List A graph). 7 Simple Graphs and the Canonical Bilinear Form (the form with values 2, $-1$, 0; Proposition 7.1 invariance; Corollary 7.2; the remark that rational trigonometry lets one work with the Tits form $Q(x,y) = 2(x^2 - xy + y^2)$ of $A_2$ without diagonalising). 8 Quadratic Forms on General Multigraphs (Lemmas 8.1 to 8.3; balanced weights and balanced multigraphs; **Theorem 8.4** and **Theorem 8.7**: an invariant form exists exactly for balanced graphs and is unique up to a constant; **Theorem 8.8**: for balanced graphs, $W$ finite iff $R(X)$ finite iff $Q$ positive definite iff spectral radius below 2 iff List B). 9 Explicit Posets of Positive Roots ($A_4$, $B_2$ to $B_4$, $C_2$ to $C_4$, $D_4$, $D_5$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $F_4$, $G_2$; the $E_8$ diagram is left "for another paper").
- What it does not contain: the numbers game beyond one sentence in the abstract and introduction (which call the mutation game dual to it and cite Mozes, Björner, Eriksson and Donnelly); any representation theory; anything on real forms. Its reference list has 31 items. One of them is Araki's 1962 paper on the root systems of symmetric spaces [Ara62], cited once in the introduction in a sentence about Dynkin's study of root systems; nothing else in the paper touches real forms.
- Naming and misprints [V: every printed list of section 9 was typed into `verify.py` and compared with the computed roots; section 2.10]: its $B_n$ for $n \ge 3$ is Bourbaki's $C_n$ with the node order reversed, its $C_n$ is Bourbaki's $B_n$ reversed, its $F_4$ is Bourbaki's $F_4$ reversed, its $G_2$ agrees with Bourbaki, and its $D_n$ and $E_n$ use an extra vertex 0. The lists for $A_4$, $B_2$ to $B_4$, $C_2$, $C_3$, $D_4$, $D_5$, $E_6$, $E_7$ and $F_4$ are correct as printed. In the $C_4$ list "1211" should read "2211"; in the $G_2$ list "12" should read "21". In Example 8.6 (the same example is on p. 20 of the manuscript, where the figure was seen) the fourth edge joins $x_4$ to $x_2$, so "$\beta_{41} = 60$" and the term $-60p_4p_1$ should read $\beta_{42}$ and $-60p_4p_2$; the corrected form is invariant under all four mutations and the printed one is not.
- List A has seventeen entries. By their printed Perron-Frobenius labels they are Kac's sixteen affine diagrams with the four-armed star entered separately from the family $\tilde D_n$; the comparison was not made edge by edge [U]. The paper's definition of a directed multigraph (section 4) allows one-way edges, and the statement of the Mutation Conjecture on p. 57 says "any multigraph"; sections 5, 6 and 9 and the theorems of section 8 assume connected bidirected graphs, while section 8 opens with a general directed multigraph (`SPEC.md` section 2.11).
- Role on the site: the citable published source for the mutation game on directed multigraphs, the classification by spectral radius, the invariant form, and the root posets.

**[W-MS]** N. J. Wildberger, "The Mutation Game, Coxeter Graphs, and Partially Ordered Multisets", unpublished manuscript, 79 pp., undated. Project file `MutationGameCoxeterGraphs.pdf`; also at <https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~norman/papers/MutationGameCoxeterGraphs.pdf> (link not re-checked).

- Status: [S] read (text and page images). A working draft: results are all numbered 0.x, there are unresolved cross-references, no abstract and no bibliography. The PDF metadata date is 25 November 2004 (read from the project file with `pdfinfo`). Wildberger cited it as "preprint (2001)" in two preprints and as "in preparation" in the published $G_2$ paper; Donnelly cites it as "preprint" with no year. Cite it as "unpublished manuscript, undated (circulated c. 2001 to 2004)". The date "2003" that appears in the documentation of the `mutation-game` Python package has no other support.
- Contents shared with [W20], under different numbers: the spectral classification (Theorem 0.5), the orders 3, 4, 6 (Theorem 0.6), the invariant form (Theorems 0.7 to 0.9), root posets (pp. 23-29). On p. 2 it poses "The Mutation Problem" for simple graphs, the earlier form of the Mutation Conjecture.
- Misprints in its root lists beyond the two it shares with [W20] [V; section 2.10]: the $A_4$ list prints 0010 twice and omits 0011; the $D_5$ list (p. 27) prints two entries twice in place of two others; the $E_6$ list (p. 28) prints three entries twice in place of three others; and the $E_6$ diagram on p. 28 is drawn with vertex 0 above vertex 2, where the lists need it above vertex 3 [S, page image]. The published paper corrects all of these.
- Contents beyond [W20]: the **dual mutation game** (p. 30: Venus, "fundamental weights", the move $r_x$, Theorem 0.10); the map $\tau$ from populations to dual populations, described as measuring the change a mutation would make at each vertex, with matrix $A - 2I$, and the adjointness $p\,s_x\tau = p\,\tau r_x$ (Proposition 0.3, pp. 31-32); increasing and incremental mutations, read off from the sign of the dual population (p. 32); the "canonical increasing sequence" on a bipartite graph (p. 33); the complementation map $\psi$ on sequences of reflections (pp. 38-43); "frames" of mutation sequences, which are heaps in Viennot's sense (pp. 45-46); change sequences and the local sum rule (Proposition 0.8, Theorem 0.13); canonical mutation frames $M(X, x)$ listed for $A_3$, $D_4$, $D_5$, $E_6$, $E_7$, $E_8$ (pp. 49-59); "root spindles" and two statements labelled Observation, given without proof (pp. 60-64); "Perron-Frobenius extensions" (pp. 72-76).
- Role on the site: the fullest account of both games together. Because it is unpublished and unfinished, statements should be cited to [W20] or [W03c] wherever those cover them.

**[W03a]** N. J. Wildberger, "A combinatorial construction for simply-laced Lie algebras", *Adv. in Appl. Math.* 30 (2003), no. 1-2, 385-396. DOI [10.1016/S0196-8858(02)00541-9](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0196-8858%2802%2900541-9). Preprint: <https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~norman/papers/CombinatorialLieAlgebras.pdf>.

- Status: [S] preprint read (the published version is on a host that refuses automated fetching). Section 3 states the mutation game for simple graphs, with the Mars story; this and [W03c] are the first *published* statements of the game. Section 3 also records that every root is non-negative or non-positive and that the author knows no direct combinatorial proof. Section 4 builds a representation on the ideals of a "minuscule heap", with raising and lowering operators $X_\alpha$, $Y_\alpha$; Theorem 4.1 asserts that these span the simple Lie algebra with a Chevalley basis. **The theorem is stated without proof**, as R. M. Green also notes in [Gre07]. $E_8$ is excluded because it has no minuscule representation.
- Role on the site: source and credit for module M8. The site must present the Chevalley-Serre relations for the game-built operators as something the reader can check on the page (and that the reference script checks), with the proof attributed to the later literature (Green's book [Gre13]), since [W03a] gives none.

**[W03b]** N. J. Wildberger, "Minuscule posets from neighbourly graph sequences", *European J. Combin.* 24 (2003), no. 6, 741-757. DOI [10.1016/S0195-6698(03)00056-8](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0195-6698%2803%2900056-8). Preprint: <https://web.maths.unsw.edu.au/~norman/papers/MinusculePosetsGraphs.pdf>. [S] preprint read. Purely graph-theoretic: classifies the graphs carrying a maximal two-neighbourly heap ($A_n$, $D_n$, $E_6$, $E_7$) and recovers Proctor's minuscule posets. Neither game appears in it.

**[W03c]** N. J. Wildberger, "A combinatorial construction of $G_2$", *J. Lie Theory* 13 (2003), no. 1, 155-165. Free published version: <https://emis.univie.ac.at/journals/JLT/vol.13_no.1/wildla.pdf>.

- Status: [S] read. Section 2, "The mutation and numbers games on a graph", is the first published definition of **both** games on directed graphs: a mutation at $z$ negates the value at $z$ and adds each neighbour's value once per edge *into* $z$; a dual mutation at $z$ negates the value at $z$ and adds it to each neighbour once per edge *out of* $z$; the numbers game allows only positive firings. The numbers game is credited to Mozes. The paper then builds the 7-dimensional representation of $\mathfrak g_2$ from a six-step numbers game and proves that the resulting operators span a 14-dimensional Lie algebra (Theorem 4.1).
- Caution: the introduction prints the dimension of $F_4$ as 56; the correct value is 52.
- Role on the site: the citation for the pair of definitions in section 2, and the worked $G_2$ example in module M8.

### 11.2 The numbers game (Mozes) and its literature

Sign convention in each entry: "fires negative" means the source's legal move is at a negative number, so its positions are the negatives of this specification's positions (section 2.9 gives the full translation table).

**[IMO86]** 27th International Mathematical Olympiad (Warsaw, 1986), Problem 3. Official sheet: <https://www.imo-official.org/assets/documents/problems/1986/1986_eng.pdf>. Solutions: <https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/1986_IMO_Problems/Problem_3>, <https://www.cut-the-knot.org/Curriculum/Algebra/IMO1986_3.shtml>.

- Status: [S] statement read on the official sheet. Five integers with positive sum sit on a pentagon; a negative middle number $y$ in a consecutive triple $x, y, z$ may be replaced by $x+y, -y, z+y$; the task is to decide whether the process must stop (it must). Fires negative.
- Role on the site: a puzzle in module M4, played in tool T15. In this specification's language it is the numbers game on the 5-cycle, which is the affine board $\tilde A_4$, started at negative level. The problem prescribes no particular numbers; the site's example start is its own.
- Open: the proposer is not named in any source that could be read [U].

**[Moz90]** S. Mozes, "Reflection processes on graphs and Weyl groups", *J. Combin. Theory Ser. A* 53 (1990), no. 1, 128-142. DOI [10.1016/0097-3165(90)90024-Q](https://doi.org/10.1016/0097-3165%2890%2990024-Q). Abstract and record: <https://cris.huji.ac.il/en/publications/reflection-processes-on-graphs-and-weyl-groups/>.

- Access: free to read in Elsevier's open archive (Elsevier user licence, which is not a Creative Commons licence). The host refuses automated fetching, so the text was **not read** for this specification.
- Status: [S] bibliographic data and abstract. [S2] everything else: later authors (Donnelly 2008; Gashi, Schedler and Speyer 2012; Bugaenko et al. 2013) report that the paper introduces the game as a generalisation of the pentagon problem, works with integer weights coming from a symmetrizable generalized Cartan matrix, fires negative numbers, proves that the length and the end position of a terminating game do not depend on the play, and describes the terminating starts through the Weyl group of a Kac-Moody algebra. Theorem numbers, the index convention of his matrix, and whether the text uses the phrase "numbers game" are unknown [U].
- Action for the build: a human should open the paper once (it is 15 pages) and fill in theorem numbers before the history page is published.

**[HEri94]** H. Eriksson, *Computational and combinatorial aspects of Coxeter groups*, PhD thesis, KTH Stockholm, 1994. <https://www.csc.kth.se/~viggo/henrik/he/thesis.pdf>. [S] first 50 pages read: a compact account (section 3) with the statement that positions represent group elements faithfully and that reduced words are exactly the plays firing only negative numbers, which solves the word problem in time linear in the word length.

**[BB05]** A. Björner, F. Brenti, *Combinatorics of Coxeter Groups*, Graduate Texts in Mathematics 231, Springer, 2005. DOI [10.1007/3-540-27596-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/3-540-27596-7). Section 4.3 "The numbers game" starts on p. 97 [S, table of contents].

- Status: section 4.3 itself was not read. Its definitions and main theorem are restated in [BCNS13, Theorem 3.3] [S2]: from a start with all entries positive, (1) two play sequences reach the same position exactly when their words are equal in the Coxeter group, (2) group elements correspond bijectively to reachable positions, (3) the descents of the element are the nodes carrying a negative number, (4) a play sequence fires only positive numbers exactly when its word is reduced. The book defines both positive and negative moves. The theorem number (believed to be 4.3.1) is [U].
- Role on the site: the standard textbook citation for module M5 and tool T8.

**[Pro84]** R. A. Proctor, "Bruhat lattices, plane partition generating functions, and minuscule representations", *European J. Combin.* 5 (1984), no. 4, 331-350. DOI [10.1016/S0195-6698(84)80037-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0195-6698%2884%2980037-2). Citation [S]; content [S2] (Donnelly): uses the firing process, with **positive** numbers fired, to compute Weyl group orbits of weights in fundamental-weight coordinates. **Two usages of the word "inner"** [V for the two lists; `verify.py` section 21]. (i) A real form is called inner when its Cartan involution is an inner automorphism, which on the roots means that $\theta$ lies in the Weyl group. This happens exactly when $\tau$ equals $-w_0$ as a symmetry of the board [V on all 167 records]. For $E_6$ these forms are the compact form, E II and E III. (ii) A real form is called inner, or of type ${}^1E_6$, when it is an inner twist of the split form, which is the case of trivial $\tau$. For $E_6$ these are E I and E IV only; the compact form is of type ${}^2E_6$, because $-w_0$ is the flip. The $\tau$ of this specification is usage (ii). Wikipedia, "E6 (mathematics)", was recorded during this research as describing the compact form as inner; the page was not read again when this paragraph was revised, and two reviewers of this specification disagreed about which usage it follows [U]. Under usage (ii) the compact form does not belong with E I and E IV. The site should say which usage it means wherever it uses the word, and should not copy the grouping from that page without re-reading it.

### 11.5 Standard Lie theory, Coxeter groups, polytopes and the A-D-E pattern

**Conventions.** The decisive fact for the build is which index of the Cartan matrix carries the coroot. SageMath and Kac use $C_{ij} = \langle\alpha_i^\vee,\alpha_j\rangle$, as this specification does. Bourbaki and Humphreys print the transpose. SageMath follows Bourbaki's node numbering; Kac numbers the E types and $G_2$ differently.

- **SageMath** documentation and source [S, read; matrices also obtained by running Sage]: <https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/thematic_tutorials/lie/lie_basics.html>, <https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/combinat/sage/combinat/root_system/cartan_type.html>, <https://doc.sagemath.org/html/en/reference/combinat/sage/combinat/root_system/cartan_matrix.html>. `CartanMatrix(['B',2]) = [[2,-1],[-2,2]]`, `['C',2] = [[2,-2],[-1,2]]`, `['G',2] = [[2,-3],[-1,2]]`, `['F',4] = [[2,-1,0,0],[-1,2,-1,0],[0,-2,2,-1],[0,0,-1,2]]`. These equal the matrices in `golden_data.json`, so **SageMath is an independent oracle that needs no translation**.

**Exponents, heights and the Coxeter element (modules M5 and M7).**

- **[Ste59]** R. Steinberg, "Finite reflection groups", *Trans. Amer. Math. Soc.* 91 (1959), 493-504. DOI [10.1090/S0002-9947-1959-0106428-2](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1959-0106428-2). Free at the AMS. [S] read (through text recognition; recheck item numbers).
